## 1. East End / Amagansett restaurant economics 2022–2026: closures, openings, vacancy, rent behavior

### Takeaway
Amagansett's food-service market turned over between 2021 and 2026, but it did not empty out. Closures were followed quickly by new operators: Hampton Chutney was followed by Doubles, Main Street Tavern by Rowdy Hall, and the 136 Main Street block was revived. By 2026 the Town Board and the local press describe Amagansett as "happening" and seeing "increasing foot traffic." Across the East End, retail vacancy is reported as low and supply as constrained, with landlords "able to choose" tenants. Off-season "dark" storefronts in East Hampton Village are seasonal closures by tenants who keep paying rent. They do not show a demand glut. The one documented Amagansett closure in 2025, Coche Comedor, was blamed on seasonal-restaurant economics, not on rent.

### Inferences
- The Amagansett record shows that food spaces which open up get re-tenanted, often by credentialed operators (NYC restaurateurs, local multi-unit groups). There is no evidence of a hamlet glut of vacant food-service space in 2024–2026.
- The Hampton Chutney episode (2021) shows an Amagansett landlord asking roughly triple the renewal rent, losing the tenant, and re-letting within about one season. This is direct local evidence that incumbents' rents can sit well below market, not above it. Doubles' rent is Unknown.
- The Coche Comedor closure supports a general claim that seasonal restaurant operation is hard. However, the same group kept La Fondita and Rowdy Hall operating in Amagansett, which suggests the decision was concept-specific.

### Inferences
- The tenant (or its predecessor) paid, or was asked to pay, up to $625,000 in key money for the 207 Main Street leasehold and equipment in 2016–2017. That is strong evidence the leasehold carried bonus value, meaning contract rent was below market rent at the time. The premises' value depends partly on the scarce Town "wet use" (food-service) entitlement, which belongs to the real estate, not to the operator.
- The chain-wide contraction (about 10 locations to 5) and the Fri–Mon hours at Amagansett point to operator-level performance and staffing choices. Those are TENANT-ECONOMICS factors. Reduced hours in a peak-tourism hamlet suggest under-utilization of the premises, not a premises defect.
- "Organic Kitchen & Eatery" appears to be the brand "Organic Krush Kitchen & Eatery." No separate rebrand entity was found. The Toast listing tag "NEW" could reflect a new POS account or a new operating entity. Unknown.

## 3. Foot traffic, tourism and the broader Hamptons economy

### Takeaway
The broad indicators were positive through mid-2026. Suffolk County sales-tax collections rose 9.2% in the first half of 2026 (third-highest growth of any NY county). Long Island grew 7.1%, the highest region outside NYC. Hamptons home prices and retail investment set records in 2026. LIRR ridership to the South Fork is reported up. Town officials cite "increasing foot traffic" on Amagansett Main Street. The weak spots are an early-2025 summer-rental slowdown and national restaurant traffic declines in 2025, concentrated among lower- and middle-income consumers. The Hamptons customer base skews toward the higher-income side of the "K-shaped" economy. No Amagansett-specific pedestrian counts exist.

### Cited Findings

## 8. Appraisal and valuation doctrine: market rent vs. tenant economics; asking vs. achieved rent

### Takeaway
Authoritative appraisal definitions define market rent as the most probable rent the property would bring in a competitive, open market between typically motivated, well-informed parties. It is a property-level concept tied to highest and best use. It is not "use rent" for a particular operator, and it is not the incumbent's ability to pay. Asking-to-achieved spreads are real and should be modeled through concessions (free rent, TI). The only granular 2026 lease data found (CompStak, Bay Area retail) shows free rent at about 2.3% of term. No Hamptons-specific asking-to-executed spread statistic was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Market rent (Dictionary of Real Estate Appraisal, 7th ed.):** "The most probable rent that a property should bring in a competitive and open market under all conditions requisite to a fair lease transaction, the lessee and lessor each acting prudently and knowledgeably, and assuming the rent is not affected by undue stimulus. Implicit in this definition is the execution of a lease as of a specified date under conditions whereby: Lessee and lessor are typically motivated; Both parties are well informed or well advised, and acting in what they consider their best interests; Payment is made in terms of cash or in terms of financial arrangements comparable thereto; and The rent reflects specified terms and conditions typically found in that market, such as permitted uses, use restrictions, expense obligations, duration, concessions, rental adjustments and revaluations, renewal and purchase options, frequency of payments (annual, monthly, etc.), and tenant improvements (TIs)." — quoted in [Diskin, Lennhoff, Parli & Roach, "Market Rent and Highest and Best Use: Joined at the Hip?", The Appraisal Journal, Spring 2022 (Appraisal Institute)](https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/getattachment/efe3dcf2-80e0-4565-97a0-9c643479e512/Market-Rent-and-Highest-and-Best-Use.pdf) (acc. 2026-10-07; B; `mkt_appraisalinstitute_market_rent_hbu_article.txt`)
- Same article: "market rent also must be based on the property's highest and best use." It proposes "use rent" (rent for a property "limited to a specific use") and says "use rent might be higher or lower than market rent ... it is never synonymous with market rent." Rents that embed non-realty components (financing, credit of a specific tenant) "are not market rent." — same source (B)
- **Market value (federal banking regulation; FIRREA definition):** "Market value means the most probable price which a property should bring in a competitive and open market under all conditions requisite to a fair sale, the buyer and seller each acting prudently and knowledgeably, and assuming the price is not affected by undue stimulus," with conditions (1)–(5), including "(1) Buyer and seller are typically motivated" and "(5) ... unaffected by special or creative financing or sales concessions." — [12 CFR § 323.2(h) (FDIC), via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/323.2) (acc. 2026-10-07; A; `mkt_lii_12cfr323-2_fdic_market_value_definition.txt`). The same definition appears at 12 CFR 34.42(h) (OCC). The eCFR fetch was blocked and that copy was not separately verified.
- **NY appellate use:** in *Panasia Estate, Inc. v Tapas y Mas, LLC*, 2024 NY Slip Op 05898, the court reportedly quoted the Dictionary (5th ed. 2010) defining market rent as reflecting "all conditions and restrictions of the lease agreement, including permitted uses ... concessions, renewal and purchase options," and noted "effective rent is a concept distinct from market rent." — [NY Courts reporter](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2024/2024_05898.htm) (acc. 2026-10-07; document grade A, but content here is a D-grade search snippet; page blocked; snippet log)
- **Asking vs. effective rent:** CompStak says asking rent is the quoted rate with no concessions, while effective rent nets out free rent and TI over the term. "Model Net Effective Rent, not asking rent, for any retail renewal." — [CompStak blog](https://compstak.com/blog/net-effective-rent-vs-asking-rent-cre-underwriting) (acc. 2026-10-07; B; `mkt_compstak_net_effective_vs_asking.txt`)
- **Retail concession and spread data (Bay Area only):** CBD retail free rent was 2.1% of term in Q4 2019, peaked at 3.4% in 2023 and fell to 2.3% in Q2 2026. Suburban retail went from 1.8% to a 3.9% peak (late 2024 / early 2025) to 2.3%. New CBD retail deals averaged $33.06/SF vs $36.30/SF in place (about −8.9%). The suburban gap was about −1.7%. — [CompStak, "Retail rent concession trends"](https://compstak.com/blog/retail-rent-concession-trends-compstak-lease-data) (acc. 2026-10-07; B; `mkt_compstak_retail_concession_trends.txt`)
- **Local historical spread:** in the 2009 Hamptons downturn, achieved rents were reported "20 or 25 percent" below flat asking rents. — [The Real Deal, June 2009](https://therealdeal.com/magazine/new-york-june-2009/retail-cools-in-hamptons) (acc. 2026-10-07; D; snippet)

### Inferences
- Under these definitions, the operator's profitability, sales slump, menu cost inflation or staffing problems do not change the market rent of 207 Main Street. They would matter only if they reflected conditions every typical food-service tenant would face at these premises, and those conditions would then show up in comparable leases. The 2016–2017 $625,000 key-money listing is market evidence that typical tenants valued this leasehold above its contract rent.
- Any market-rent opinion (a broker opinion of value or an appraisal) should compare the subject's $54.55/SF/yr (gross or net unknown) against achieved Amagansett and East End food-service leases adjusted for concessions. It should not compare against asking rents alone. Where only asking data exist, a concession or spread adjustment is appropriate. The size of that adjustment for the Hamptons in 2026 is Unknown. Low reported vacancy in 2025–2026 suggests it is smaller than in 2009.

## 9. Evaluation matrix: anticipated tenant arguments

For each argument: (a) what the evidence shows; (b) MARKET-RENT factor (affects what any typical tenant would pay for these premises) or TENANT-ECONOMICS factor (specific to this operator); (c) the strongest evidence-based landlord response. Sources are as cited in sections 1–8.

### Takeaway
Of the ten expected arguments, only building condition, limited square footage and parking are potentially market-rent factors. All three are fixed, known attributes that existed when the leasehold commanded key money and that comparable-rent analysis already prices. Struggling business, seasonal revenue, cost inflation and competition are tenant-economics or industry-wide factors. General economic conditions and foot traffic are market factors, but the 2025–2026 evidence on both favors the landlord. Construction disruption was temporary, targeted at the opposite side of the street (subject to confirmation), and pre-season.

### Cited Findings
**1. "Our business is struggling."**
- (a) The chain shrank from about 10 locations (2020) to 5 (2026) after closures in VA, CT, Stony Brook and New Hyde Park. Amagansett runs Fri–Mon only in fall 2026 and closes in winter. No bankruptcy was found. ([Richmond BizSense 2023](https://richmondbizsense.com/2023/09/18/organic-krush-restaurant-closes-short-pump-outpost-after-4-years/), C; [Richmond BizSense 2024](https://richmondbizsense.com/2024/06/17/organic-krush-closes-at-ur-campus-new-eatery-to-open-in-its-place/), C; [organickrush.com](https://organickrush.com/), C; Yelp/Toast, D)
- (b) **TENANT-ECONOMICS.**
- (c) Market rent is "the most probable rent that a property should bring ... lessee and lessor each ... typically motivated" ([Appraisal Journal 2022](https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/getattachment/efe3dcf2-80e0-4565-97a0-9c643479e512/Market-Rent-and-Highest-and-Best-Use.pdf), B). The same 1,100 SF space was marketed with $625,000 key money and drew "several serious inquiries" in one day ([27east 2016](https://www.27east.com/real-estate-news/marys-marvelous-in-amagansett-to-close-when-new-tenant-is-found-1404430), C). Spaces that came open in Amagansett 2021–2026 were re-let (Doubles, Rowdy Hall, 136 Main Street). Ask the tenant to produce sales and P&L before discussing a temporary, repayable deferral rather than a permanent reduction.

**2. "Revenue is seasonal."**
- (a) Seasonality is structural and long-standing. Organic Krush closed December–March as early as 2019. Coche Comedor cited seasonal-restaurant difficulty in 2025. ([Dan's Papers 2019](https://www.danspapers.com/2019/11/healthy-organic-krush), D; [EH Star 2025](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/food/2025925/adios-coche-comedor), C)
- (b) Seasonality is a **MARKET** condition common to every Hamptons tenant. That makes it a factor **already embedded** in market rents and in what the tenant paid for the leasehold in 2017. It is not a new change.
- (c) The tenant knowingly bought into a seasonal hamlet. The founder was a Montauk summer resident ([27east 2017](https://27east.com/east-hampton-press/marys-marvelous-leaves-amagansett-organic-krush-to-move-in-1633346), C). Market rents for seasonal locations already reflect "three hot months." Seasonality has not changed adversely. If anything, the season is lengthening: year-round Penn–Montauk trains started September 2026 (D), and East Hampton Village vacancies have been low since COVID ([WWD 2026](https://wwd.com/business-news/retail/hamptons-retail-scene-whats-new-for-summer-2026-1238932696/), B).

**3. "Construction / disruption hurt us."**
- (a) The 2026 brick-sidewalk project ran on the **south** side, Hedges Lane to the Amagansett Square west entrance, targeted for May 1–15, 2026. The subject appears to be on the **north** side (OSM geocode, D; 203 Main Street on the north side, C). No water-main, utility or NY-27 roadwork affecting the frontage was found. The lighting project is still in engineering. ([EH Star March 2026](https://easthamptonstar.com/government/202635/amagansett-sidewalk-beautification-works), C; [27east March 2026](https://www.27east.com/east-hampton-press/news/government/article_2c6df056-ba7d-486e-b086-00d254e2632f.html), C; [EH Star Nov. 2025](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/government/20251113/new-lights-near-amagansetts-main-street), C)
- (b) Temporary public works are at most a **short-term abatement question under lease terms**. They are not a permanent MARKET-RENT factor. The finished project is a market-rent **positive** (wider sidewalks, "improve access to businesses and increase foot traffic").
- (c) Ask the tenant to document specific dates of obstruction at its frontage. Point out that the work was timed before Memorial Day, when the store is already on a reduced schedule, and was located across the street. Town officials justified it with "increasing foot traffic." Any accommodation should be a one-time credit tied to proven days of obstruction, not a base-rent cut.

**4. "Labor and food cost inflation."**
- (a) Long Island minimum wage went $16.50 (2025) to $17.00 (2026), and the tipped food-service cash wage to $11.35 ([NYS DOL P717](https://dol.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/p717_12-25.pdf), A). Wages are **frozen at $17.00 for 2027** because the off-ramp was triggered ([NYS DOL](https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-0), A). Food-away-from-home CPI is about +3.4% year over year to August 2026 (D). 87% of operators report food-cost increases (D).
- (b) **Industry-wide TENANT-ECONOMICS.** These are operating costs every competing operator bears. They affect market rent only as reflected in comparable leases. They are not a defect of these premises.
- (c) Costs are passed through in menu pricing ("more than half of operators raised their menu prices," D). The 2027 wage freeze ends the wage-escalation trend the tenant may project forward. Market rent for a scarce "wet use" space depends on what the next operator would pay, and the 2026 entrants (Sett, Jamagansett, Amagansett Coffee Co.) chose Amagansett despite the same costs.

**5. "Foot traffic is down."**
- (a) Official 2026 statements report "increasing foot traffic" and "Amagansett is happening right now." The West End "business boom" is documented for summer 2026. Suffolk sales tax was +9.2% in H1 2026 (A). LIRR South Fork ridership is reported up (D). No pedestrian-count data exists. Nationally, more than 60% of operators saw 2025 traffic declines, concentrated in lower-income segments (B).
- (b) Foot traffic is a **MARKET-RENT** factor, but the local evidence runs against the tenant.
- (c) Ask the tenant to substantiate with its own transaction counts, then show that the hamlet's traffic rose. Any decline in its own counts points to operator factors, such as hours cut to Fri–Mon and new café competition. Cite [EH Star July 2026](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/villages-business/202672/amagansetts-west-end-sees-business-boom) (C), [27east March 2026](https://www.27east.com/east-hampton-press/news/government/article_2c6df056-ba7d-486e-b086-00d254e2632f.html) (C) and [NYS OSC July 2026](https://www.osc.ny.gov/press/releases/2026/07/dinapoli-local-sales-tax-collections-total-127-billion-first-half-2026-68-over-last-year) (A).

**6. "The building is in poor condition."**
- (a) No public evidence about the current condition of 207 Main Street was found. The 2016 marketing described a one-story 1,100 SF building with kitchen, outdoor storage and patio, plus equipment that conveyed. — Unknown
- (b) Physical condition is a **MARKET-RENT** factor only to the extent it falls below comparable premises and is the landlord's responsibility under the lease. Deferred maintenance that is the tenant's obligation is not.
- (c) Check the lease's repair and maintenance allocation. Document condition with a dated inspection and photos. If any capital items are landlord obligations, address them directly (repair) rather than through a permanent rent cut. Note the historic district limits alterations for all comparable Main Street buildings alike.

**7. "The space is small (about 1,100 SF)."**
- (a) Size was disclosed in 2016 marketing and was fixed when the tenant acquired the leasehold. The CB outdoor-dining rule allows up to 100% of indoor seating outdoors (D). The premises include a patio. ([27east 2016](https://www.27east.com/real-estate-news/marys-marvelous-in-amagansett-to-close-when-new-tenant-is-found-1404430), C)
- (b) Size is a **MARKET-RENT** attribute, but it is handled in a per-SF comparable analysis. Small spaces typically command **higher** rent per SF, and a known size is not a changed circumstance.
- (c) $5,000/month is about $54.55/SF/yr. Unverified East Hampton Village asking rents run $77–$150/SF (D). The patio extends usable seasonal area. The tenant already priced size into its 2017 purchase.

**8. "Parking is limited."**
- (a) Long-standing merchant complaints led the Town to expand the municipal lot north of Main Street to about 185 spaces (2020 plan). Additional public parking is planned on the adjoining Field property (2024 plan). ([EH Star 2020](https://easthamptonstar.com/government/2020123/more-parking-amagansett-memorial-day), C; [27east 2024](https://27east.com/east-hampton-press/a-new-public-park-and-parking-for-amagansett-2286972), C)
- (b) **MARKET-RENT** attribute of the location, shared by all Main Street comparables. It is improving, not deteriorating.
- (c) Parking capacity has grown since the tenant entered in 2017. The constraint was known and priced into the 2016–2017 leasehold sale. A walk-up, take-out-oriented concept is the least parking-dependent use.

**9. "There is more competition."**
- (a) New entrants since 2022 include Doubles, Bonfire, Rowdy Hall (relocated), Meeting House (reopened), Sett Coffee, Amagansett Coffee Co. and Jamagansett. No new organic juice or acai competitor was documented. (Sections 1 and 6)
- (b) Mainly **TENANT-ECONOMICS** (concept positioning). Competitive entry also signals **MARKET** demand, which supports rent.
- (c) New capital entering the hamlet shows that market participants value Amagansett food and beverage locations. Competition is normal for any location. A tenant's loss of share to rivals is not a property defect.

**10. "General economic conditions."**
- (a) H1 2026: Suffolk sales tax +9.2%, Long Island +7.1% (A). Q1 2026 Hamptons median home price +18%, luxury +30% (B). Record $39M Hamptons retail sale in May 2026 (B). East End retail vacancy is low with "bidding wars" (B). Caveats: the Comptroller warns growth is partly inflation and tariffs and "may not last" (A). Early-2025 summer rentals were reported down about 30% (D). National restaurant traffic was weak in 2025 (B).
- (b) **MARKET-RENT** factor, and on current evidence it supports holding rent.
- (c) The local economy at the tenant's customer base (affluent seasonal residents) is strong by every primary indicator found. Any softness is national and income-skewed.

### Inferences
- A defensible landlord position: no permanent reduction to contract rent, because the evidence identifies no adverse change in market-rent factors since the lease began. Short-term accommodations, if granted, should be framed as tenant-economics relief: deferral with payback, a one-time credit for documented construction days, or a rent-for-term or financial-disclosure trade. They should not be framed as a market-rent reset.
- The strongest single market fact for the landlord is the documented $625,000 key-money valuation of this exact leasehold (2016–2017), combined with scarce "wet use" entitlement and low 2025–2026 East End retail vacancy.
