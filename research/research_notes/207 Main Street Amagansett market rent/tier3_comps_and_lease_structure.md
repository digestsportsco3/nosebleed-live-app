# Tier 3 Comparables (Hamptons outside Amagansett) and Hamptons Lease-Structure / Normalization Framework — for 207 Main Street, Amagansett

Prepared 2026-10-07. Scope: Town of East Hampton and Town of Southampton excluding Amagansett. Amagansett items that turned up are labeled "OUT OF SCOPE – hand to Amagansett researcher" and kept only where they are the best seasonal-vs-annual evidence.

Conventions used on every line: ASKING vs EXECUTED; ANNUAL vs SEASONAL; NNN vs GROSS vs UNSTATED. $/SF/yr is computed by me as (annual rent ÷ SF) unless the source states it. "Mod." = the WordPress dateModified of the broker listing page (best available listing date). All accessed 2026-10-07. Source grades: A = executed/recorded/government/broker-confirmed completed deal; B = current broker listing with detailed lease terms or established real-estate publication; C = historical listing, local newspaper, business website; D = aggregator, unverified database, search snippet.

Methodology note: LoopNet, Crexi, CityFeet, Showcase, Realmo, BizQuest and realty.com all returned HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and curl, so figures from those sites come only from search-engine snippets (grade D). The primary listing source that was fully readable was the Hamptons Commercial Real Estate team (Hal Zwick / Jeffrey Sztorc / Sean Deery, Compass), hamptonscommercialre.com ("HCRE"), which is the listing broker on most of the Village storefronts that also appear on LoopNet. Duplicate syndications were collapsed into the HCRE line.

## 1. Current (Oct 2026) restaurant and small-retail lease listings by node

### Takeaway
I found 30 or so current or recent asking-rent data points outside Amagansett. Only a few are restaurant or food-service spaces with stated rent: 17 S Edison St, Montauk (2,729 SF turnkey restaurant, $156,000/yr + NNN = $57.16/SF NNN, plus $250,000 key money); 62 Montauk Hwy, Westhampton ($132,000 year-1 + NNN on 4,400 SF = $30.00/SF, plus $350,000 key money); a Napeague Stretch restaurant (4,476 SF, $180,000 base = $40.21/SF, Oct 2024); and a Hampton Bays bar/restaurant (1,700 SF, $6,500/mo = $45.88/SF, undated). The small-storefront comps in the core villages are almost all quoted "+ NNN" and fall into two groups. (a) Trophy or luxury-corridor space in East Hampton Village core, Southampton Main Street and small Sag Harbor Main Street units runs about $125–$240/SF NNN. (b) Secondary or off-core, larger or multi-level, highway, or food-oriented space runs about $40–$90/SF NNN.

### Inferences
- **Two-tier market.** The village-core small-shop luxury tier (EH Village Circle/Park Pl/Main/Newtown, Southampton Main, small Sag Harbor Main units, Wainscott highway boutique) clusters at **~$125–$190/SF NNN**, and up to $240/SF for units of 400–500 SF. Restaurant and food-service space with stated rent clusters at **~$30–$57/SF base (NNN or unstated)**, and is usually paired with **key money of $250,000–$350,000** (excluding Amagansett). Restaurant tenants pay less per SF because restaurant spaces are larger (2,700–4,500 SF) and include back-of-house. Brokers price them by seats ("restaurants are valued on the basis of the number of tables", Zwick 2016, see Q3), and turnkey value is captured separately through key money.
- **Key money as rent.** Amortizing key money adds materially to effective rent. Straight-line over an assumed 10-year term with no discount rate (my assumption):
  - 17 S Edison: $250,000 / 10 / 2,729 SF = +$9.16/SF, so ~$66/SF effective NNN.
  - 62 Montauk Hwy: $350,000 / 10 / 4,400 SF = +$7.95/SF, so ~$38/SF.
  - Key money often pays for equipment or an existing operator's business rather than going to the landlord, so treat it as an upper-bound adjustment.
- **Closest food-service comps.** For a ~1,100 SF one-story Main Street food-service building, the closest comps by use are 17 S Edison (Montauk) and 17 Windmill Ln (Southampton; rent not published). The closest by size and format are the small village storefronts of 700–1,600 SF, which are retail and mostly luxury-corridor. The subject's $54.55/SF sits at the top of the non-Amagansett restaurant range ($30–57 base) and far below the small-shop luxury tier ($125–$190).
- **Size effect.** Smaller units command higher $/SF: 117 Main SH at 400 SF is $240/SF versus 102 Main SH at 2,600 SF at $129/SF. The 1997 East Hampton Star likewise notes that "under 1,000 square feet costs proportionately more". A 1,100 SF subject should be compared with 700–1,600 SF comps, or size-adjusted.

## 4. Seasonal leasing: examples, premium, and effective annualized rent

### Takeaway
Seasonal (summer-only) commercial leases are documented throughout 2009–2026 and still exist in 2026 (marina shops, a Hampton Bays canal store, an Amagansett restaurant, a Sag Harbor end-cap). Seasonal rent for 4–6 months has run at **about 2/3 to 3/4 of the annual asking rent**, with ~73% in a 2026 restaurant example. On an annualized per-month-of-occupancy basis, that is a **premium of roughly +33% to +125%** over annual rent. Landlords prefer annual leases because seasonal tenants leave stores empty in winter. No documented winter concessions for year-round tenants were found.

### Inferences — effective annualized rent computations
Formula: **effective annualized rent = total contractual rent ÷ months of occupancy × 12.** The season length is not defined in any listing, so I show 4 months (Memorial Day–Labor Day rounded up), 5 months (May–Sept) and 6 months (May–Oct). All are asking figures.

| Example | Contract rent | Months | Annualized | vs. annual comparator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 74A Montauk Hwy, Amagansett (OUT OF SCOPE) — seasonal $175,000 vs annual $240,000 (2,830 SF) | $175,000 | 4 / 5 / 6 | $525,000 / $420,000 / $350,000 ($185.51 / $148.41 / $123.67 per SF) | Seasonal = **72.9%** of annual base for 33–50% of the year → **+119% / +75% / +46%** per occupied month. The annual option also requires $700k key money (which buys equipment). At $70k/yr over 10 years, the annual cost is $310,000, so seasonal = 56% of annual all-in. |
| 2017 broker rule (2/3–3/4 of annual for 4–6 months) | 0.667–0.75 × annual | 4–6 | 1.33× to 2.25× annual | **+33% to +125%** per occupied month |
| 2015 Bridgehampton month-to-month | $10,000/mo | any | $120,000 | 0% premium per month vs. the $120,000 annual |
| 6 Tepee St, Hampton Bays (800 SF) | $36,000 | 4 / 5 | $108,000 / $86,400 ($135 / $108 per SF) | vs. Hampton Bays restaurant annual ~$46/SF (different use; D) |
| Shagwong marina shop (400 SF) | $30,000 | 4 / 5 | $90,000 / $72,000 ($225 / $180 per SF) | No annual comparator |
| 2016 CFO quote, East Hampton | $200–250k | 3 | $800k–$1.0M | Same dollars as 9 months in Manhasset |

- For a market-rent opinion on an annual lease, seasonal quotes should be divided by ~0.67–0.75, not annualized pro rata by month, to estimate the equivalent annual rent. Pro-rata annualization overstates annual market rent by 33–125%.
- A landlord accepting an annual tenant gives up the seasonal premium in exchange for 12 months of income and continuous occupancy. Brokers consistently say landlords prefer this (2016, 2017).

### Gaps
- No documented case of a year-round tenant getting reduced winter rent (a seasonal rent schedule) in the Hamptons. The only concession evidence is 2009 "teaser rates".
- No executed seasonal rent with a defined occupancy window (e.g., May 15–Sept 30) was found; season length is assumed.
- No Montauk, Southampton or Sag Harbor restaurant with both a seasonal and an annual quote for the same space was found outside Amagansett.
