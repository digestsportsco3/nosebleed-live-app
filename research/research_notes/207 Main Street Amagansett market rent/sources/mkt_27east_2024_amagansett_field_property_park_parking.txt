The draft plan of the former Field property. COURTESY EAST HAMPTON TOWN

A draft management plan for a tract of land immediately to the northeast of the municipal parking lot in Amagansett, which calls for 1.6 of the parcel’s 2 acres to become a park, was presented to the East Hampton Town Board on Tuesday, September 3.

The property formerly known as the Field property, once the site of a dairy farm, is presently overgrown with vegetation, including invasive species. It was most recently owned by the late Herbert Field. A contract by which the town was to acquire the property was created in 2016, but not executed until last year.

The plan calls for a public-use park, said Michael Jordan, a co-chair of the town’s Nature Preserve Committee, and possibly a community meeting space. The property will be open 365 days a year from sunrise to sunset, Jordan told the board.

“There will be an ADA-compliant sidewalk from the municipal lot to the preserve, and that will then be extended over to where the restrooms are located at the back of the Amagansett parking lot,” he said. “There will be a multi-use trail installed on this property,” most likely handicapped-accessible and with a firm surface such as crushed stone. Potential additions, he said, include a playground, chess tables, benches and a shelter with picnic tables. Plans also call for fencing, possibly new plantings, and a kiosk at the entrance with any rules and regulations for the park.

The portion not dedicated to a park, which abuts the existing parking lot, will become additional public parking at a date to be determined, Jordan said. Municipal funds accounted for 30 percent of the property’s cost, with Community Preservation Fund money accounting for the remainder, allowing the dual uses of parking and parkland.

Pets will be prohibited, Jordan said, given the property’s small size. It’s going to be basically grass and park, with kids wandering around,” he said. “I don’t think we want any excrement from dogs or other pets on the property,” he added, worrying aloud about plastic bags filled with waste “that will be there when I’m long gone.”

Supervisor Kathee Burke-Gonzalez asked if a playground should be installed. “My personal view is not at this time,” Jordan said, citing the existence of a playground at nearby Amber Waves Farm. Another playground is at the Amagansett School.

“The recommendation of the Nature Preserve Committee was for the allowance to have one, not necessarily that there’s going to be one,” Councilman David Lys said.

There was also discussion of creating a walking trail, despite the property’s small size. “It’s worth it to potentially have access for ADA use,” Lys said. “It gives an opportunity for individuals with disabilities to go onto public properties.”

Burke-Gonzalez said that the board should determine whether the Amagansett Citizens Advisory Committee supports the plan. Councilman Tom Flight, the Town Board’s liaison to the committee, will discuss it with the committee when it meets on Monday evening.

“It will be interesting to hear back what the community wants there,” the supervisor said.
