The Vacant Storefront That Held My Dream An Imperfect Beginning Nov 12, 2024 12 4 1 Share A digital watercolor of Mary’s Marvelous in Amagansett, NY, given to me by a customer, Kathy Rupff For two years I drove past the vacant corner storefront, almost every day, on my way to somewhere else.
I didn’t know it was perfect.
I only knew I couldn’t stop noticing it.
The slight lopsidedness of the small wooden building charmed me.
It had character—personality—as if it were alive.
Something about its two large pane-glass windows felt magical.
When I peered inside, I sensed the soul of the place waiting.
It kept calling to me, though I couldn’t have said why.
I couldn’t ignore it.
I couldn’t forget it.
That little cottage storefront became the answer I had been searching for, a place of my own, where I could dive into what I loved: the simple, steady work of feeding people.
I knew it was time to begin.
I had carried this desire for years—delaying it, keeping it tucked inside— until it pressed outward, asking at last to be lived.
Mary’s Marvelous came into being in a not-so-traditional way.
With nothing more than my promise and a handshake on a lease, 207 Main Street became my neighborhood and my office.
This singular place in Amagansett held everything I needed— an improbable fit.
Five minutes down the road was my favorite beach.
From the shop’s front stoop, I could hear the ocean, feel the salt air, and smell washed-up seaweed.
Through the kitchen’s back door, I often stepped onto a path that carried me and my dog, Jake, through a maze of summer corn.
This was my new normal.
This was my paradise.
The comical part is that my vision was clouded.
I thought the building was charming— and it was, in its way— but little did I know how much attention that charm would require.
There were years when I felt that if I blew hard enough, the whole place might slowly tip over.
We were always repairing something.
The building had problems.
Real ones.
For a place that always seemed on the verge of needing repair, the store carried an improbable abundance.
So much food, so many days, so many people passing through.
Its imperfect charm—and I may be giving it too much credit— felt, somehow, like part of the reason it was loved.
Who could have imagined that in this tiny 1,100-square-foot building— with a kitchen not much bigger than a bathroom— my new team and I would bake and cook the days away?
I had no sense then of what would come of it.
Only that it was time to begin.
From my table to your, Mary
