Come Memorial Day, Main Street in Amagansett will sport a fresh look, with the plan essentially being to copy the sidewalk design from the north side of the street and install it on the southern side.

Lining the north side of Main Street is a brick walkway that connects the sidewalk to the street. On the southern side of Main Street, meanwhile, the sidewalk and street are connected via a thin strip of grass that is frequently worn down by foot traffic.

This new plan, then, would see the brick design that lines the northern curb on Main Street copied onto the southern curb, replacing the narrow grass landscape. This would run from Hedges Lane to the western entrance of Amagansett Square.

“Let's be honest: Amagansett is happening right now,” said Councilman David Lys, who brought forward the designs. “It is a wonderful hamlet, historical hamlet – great stores, great activity and the sidewalk there right now is not the width for the use of it. People want to walk side-by-side, and you can see the trip hazards that have worn out.”

East Hampton Town Councilman David Lys.

 Jack Motz

What the plan, which will be funded through a state grant, also calls for, besides supplanting the grass with brick, is widening the existing sidewalk from 4 to 5 feet. This is because the sidewalk struggles to accommodate people walking side-by-side, and the existing grass streetscape gets worn out quickly. The change would bring it in line with the sidewalks in other central business districts.

Another piece of the puzzle is creating wood boxes to enclose the trees, which would again mirror the design of the north side of the street, as a way of protecting the trees that line the southern side.

Construction is slated to begin this spring; town officials are targeting a completion date of between May 1 and May 15, just before Memorial Day.

At a work session discussion on Tuesday, all on the East Hampton Town Board offered support for the move, with the consensus being that leaving the grass would be ideal, but the brick layout is more practical for that space.

“Yes, it would be lovely to have grass there, but with the increasing foot traffic we’re seeing there, plus with the kind of ecological setup there – in summer, when the trees are in full bloom, there’s not that much light to help the grass thrive, it’s not a great environment for grass to grow – so you’re always going to have these problems,” said Councilman Tom Flight, who is the hamlet liaison for Amagansett.

Councilwoman Cate Rogers largely agreed with the others, but she made note that preserving history and ensuring the sidewalk is safe are not at odds with one another.

“Brick is something that fits in with the historic nature, and I would hate to see concrete all the way to the curb as a replacement,” Rogers said. “I think that would impact the historic nature and really take that small town feeling away that that we all love so much and is why we live here.”

From the public, reaction was split. Dan Mongan welcomed the idea of repairing the footpaths, which he said need attention. But he was hesitant about putting in a brick design to replace the grass.

“What’s not great for me is taking grass out and putting in hard pavement,” Mongan said. “I think you have options. I think you have less expensive and more beautiful options. So in particular, the idea of bringing the design philosophy of the north side of the street, at that section, to the south side, I think, is undesirable. From my opinion, the hamlet has a variety of different architectural mini-environments, and those two sides of the street are quite different. The north side is more densely developed. It’s more commercial. It’s more heavily used. Everything about it is busier.”

Dan Mongan addresses the Town Board on Tuesday.

 Jack Motz

Meanwhile, Michael Cinque, a business owner with a shop on the northern side of the street, offered his support for the measure.

“Being on Main Street for over 45 years, I’m here calling to support the beautification and also the safety of the south side of Main Street,” Cinque said. “It’s just absolutely going to make it beautiful. Right now, most of the year it’s either mud or dirt.”
