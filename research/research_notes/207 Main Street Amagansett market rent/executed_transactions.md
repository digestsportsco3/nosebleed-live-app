# Executed Commercial Lease Transactions and Historical Rents — Amagansett / Town of East Hampton / Sag Harbor / Southampton (2014–2026), for 207 Main Street, Amagansett

Researcher notes. Date accessed for every URL below: 2026-10-07 unless stated. Subject: 207 Main Street, Amagansett, about 1,100 SF, one-story food-service building, currently rented at $5,000/month ($60,000/yr = $54.55/SF/yr).

Source grades: A = executed lease, recorded transaction, government record, broker-confirmed completed transaction; B = current broker listing with detailed terms / established real estate publication; C = historical listing, local newspaper, business website; D = aggregator, unverified database, search-result snippet.

**Headline finding:** I could not find a single publicly documented executed rent (dollar rent per month, per year, or per SF) for any Amagansett commercial space, 2014–2026. The best Amagansett transaction evidence is about the **subject property itself**. In 2016–2017 the long-term lease at 207 Main Street, with its equipment, was marketed for **$625,000 in key money**, later quoted as "listed for $575K". The assignment to Organic Krush **closed on June 12, 2017** at an undisclosed price. The contract rent under that lease was never published. Elsewhere in the Town of East Hampton, the only executed rents with dollar figures come from Traded.co deal records for three East Hampton Village retail leases (2021–2024; $73–$145/SF). Those records are aggregator-grade, and I could not open the pages directly (HTTP 403).

## 1. Executed lease rents documented for Amagansett commercial space (Main Street / Montauk Highway)

### Takeaway
No executed Amagansett rent has been disclosed in any public source I could reach. The only documented completed Amagansett lease transaction with a dollar figure is the 2017 assignment of the 207 Main Street lease itself (Mary's Marvelous to Organic Krush). Its key money was asked at $625K and later quoted as "listed for $575K"; the deal closed with the price undisclosed. That a buyer paid key money for this lease is strong evidence that its contract rent was well below market for a scarce "wet use" Main Street food site.

### Inferences
- **The 2017 key money shows the subject lease was below market.** A buyer will pay ~$575K–$625K (less the unknown value of used equipment) to step into an existing lease only if its rent is well below what a new lease at market would cost, or if the use permit itself is scarce. Illustration only, with my own assumptions (remaining term and discount rate were never disclosed): capitalizing $575K at 10% over 10, 15 or 20 remaining years implies a below-market rent advantage of about $93,600, $75,600 or $67,500 per year ($7,800, $6,300 or $5,600 per month). At $625K the figures are $101,700, $82,200 or $73,400 per year. Because part of the premium pays for equipment and the scarce wet-use status, these numbers are a loose upper-bound indicator of the gap, not a measurement.
- If the current $5,000/month is the same (assigned) long-term lease that Organic Krush bought in 2017, it is almost certainly a legacy below-market rent and should not be read as evidence of market rent. I could not confirm whether the current $5,000/month lease is the original assigned lease or a new one.
- 177 Main St (Rowdy Hall) is the closest physical comp: a Main Street Amagansett restaurant leased in 2023 to an institutional landlord, with rent undisclosed. As a purely illustrative cross-check, a 5.5–6.5% cap rate on the $4.45M price implies NOI of ~$245K–$289K, about $84–$100/SF on 2,900 SF. That figure is inferred from a sale price, not a lease, and the buyer may have priced in upside.
- The Hampton Chutney episode (Amagansett Square renewal offered at "almost triple" the prior rent in 2021) documents a post-pandemic step-up in Amagansett Square restaurant rents of roughly 3x for a tenant of 20+ years. Absolute numbers are not public.

### Gaps
- No contract rent, term, escalations or lease type for 207 Main Street (2002 lease, 2017 assignment, or current) is published anywhere I could reach. The original Douglas Elliman listing is not archived.
- No executed rent for Amagansett Square, 136 Main St, 11 Indian Wells Hwy, 177 Main St (Rowdy Hall), 4 Amagansett Square (Wölffer/Christian's), Astro's, Jack's Stir Brew, Hamptons Coffee, Cavaniola's, Amagansett Wine & Spirits, Lazy Point or Meeting House could be found. Searches for Astro's, Jack's, Hampton Coffee and Mandala returned no rent reporting.
- I found no recorded memorandum of lease for any Amagansett restaurant. NY RPL §291-c allows recording for terms over 3 years, but Suffolk County Clerk land records are not searchable through public web search.

### Inferences
- The executed East Hampton Village retail rents ($73–$145/SF in 2022–2024) are for dry retail on prime village blocks. On cost-of-occupancy grounds they are not directly transferable to a 1,100 SF Amagansett food-service building, but they bracket the top of the Town's retail rent range. The $145/SF NNN figure at 53 The Circle (2024) has the best internal support.
- Asking key money for Hamptons restaurant leases has clustered at roughly 2.7–2.9x the asking annual base rent: 74A Montauk Hwy $700K/$240K = 2.9x; Westhampton $350K/$132K = 2.7x; Hampton Bays $500K/$180K = 2.8x. These are asking-only ratios. They show that key money of $500K–$700K is typical for turnkey wet-use restaurant leases in this market, which puts the 2017 207 Main Street figure ($575K–$625K for only 1,100 SF) at the high end per SF.

## 3. Hamptons restaurants sold as businesses with the lease rent disclosed

### Takeaway
None found within the Town of East Hampton, Sag Harbor or Southampton. Business-for-sale and "lease for sale" listings for Hamptons restaurants consistently disclose the key money or lease price but withhold the underlying rent. The only business listings that disclose rent are in Westhampton Beach and elsewhere in Suffolk County, outside the target area.

## 5. Historical trend: 2015–2019 vs. 2021–2026

### Takeaway
Only qualitative and asking-level evidence exists. It points to a soft, discount-prone market in 2016–2019 (especially Southampton), followed by a sharp post-pandemic step-up (2021–2024): Amagansett Square renewal "almost triple", East End retail rents +9.8% year over year in 2022 and +62% over a decade, and East Hampton Village executed rents of $125–$145/SF by 2022–2024. Seasonality-driven turnover in Amagansett resumed by 2023–2025.

### Cited Findings
- 2016–2017: Southampton landlords complained of tenants wanting "Champagne on a beer budget" (2016); more offers at asking in 2017; Main St $140–150/SF — [The Real Deal, July 2017](https://therealdeal.com/magazine/new-york-july-2017/fashion-and-furnishings-lead-a-retail-revival-in-southampton/)
- 2019: Southampton asking rents fell from ~$110 to $85 (Main) and $50 (Jobs Lane); landlords "responding with discounts"; Sag Harbor, Amagansett and Bridgehampton "saw little turnover" — [The Real Deal, June 3, 2019](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/tristate/2019/06/03/revolving-retail-in-the-hamptons/)
- 2017 (subject): a 1,100 SF Amagansett food-use lease commanded $575K–$625K in key money — [27east](https://27east.com/east-hampton-press/marys-marvelous-leaves-amagansett-organic-krush-to-move-in-1633346)
- 2021: Amagansett Square renewal offered at "almost triple" the prior rent (lease expired Oct 2020) — [EH Star, May 27, 2021](https://easthamptonstar.com/villages/2021527/hampton-chutney-co-squeezed-out-square)
- 2021: East Hampton Village Main/Newtown average $125–$150/SF — [EH Star, Sept 2, 2021](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/villages/202192/general-store-east-hampton-its-challenge)
- 2022: East End retail rent ~$46/SF, +9.8% over 12 months, +62.2% vs. 10 years earlier — [Behind The Hedges, Nov 8, 2022](https://behindthehedges.com/southampton-restaurant-sale-first-time-20/)
- 2022–2024 executed (aggregator): East Hampton Village $73, $125 and $145/SF — Traded.co (Section 2)
- Amagansett commercial building values roughly doubled: 177 Main St $2.825M (2020) to $4.45M (2023) — [Behind The Hedges](https://behindthehedges.com/final-figure-amagansett-restaurant-sells-4-5m). 136 Main + 11 Indian Wells bought for $5.6M + $5.2M (2021–22), assemblage asked at $19M in 2026 — [EH Star, July 2, 2026](https://easthamptonstar.com/villages-business/202672/amagansetts-west-end-sees-business-boom)
- 2023–2025 Amagansett: Main Street "in flux"; winter closures; Coche Comedor (74A Montauk Hwy) closed in 2025 citing seasonality; 74A re-offered at $240K/yr + $700K key money in 2026 — [EH Star, Feb 16, 2023](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/villages-business/2023216/amagansett-main-street-flux); [EH Star, Sept 25, 2025](https://www.easthamptonstar.com/food/2025925/adios-coche-comedor); [Hamptons Commercial RE](https://hamptonscommercialre.com/properties/iconic-amagansett-restaurant-turn-key-opportunity/)

### Inferences
- Taken together, post-2020 Amagansett/East Hampton restaurant and retail rents appear to have reset materially upward, perhaps 2x–3x for renewing legacy tenants, judging by the Hampton Chutney "almost triple" and the building-value doubling. A lease negotiated in 2002, or assigned in 2017, would likely sit far below 2025–2026 market.

### Gaps
- No time series of executed Amagansett rents exists in public sources. Any quantified trend must rely on asking rents, broker opinion, or CoStar data that is not publicly accessible.
