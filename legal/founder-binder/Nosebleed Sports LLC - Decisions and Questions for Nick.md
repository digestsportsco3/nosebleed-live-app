5. **Brock's $800 a month and bonuses are JGN's own expense**, not added to your capital, unless JGN and the company write down that a payment was for the platform. You did not list them among the platform's costs, so I left them out of the $12,990.

7. **Seven months of charges.** I counted May through November as seven monthly charges. If November's had not been charged by closing, the total is lower. JGN also has 60 days after closing to add anything missed. The $500 Delaware filing and registered-agent cost is now in the list.

## 3. Glover: out of the binder, and what that does and does not change

**Why he was in it.** He was never an owner of Nosebleed Sports LLC. He was in the binder because JGN owned the code and the brand while he was a JGN member, and because the agreement you signed with him on November 11 limits what JGN can do with the brand, the social accounts and its receipts until his $12,600 is paid (final date December 31, 2027).

**What v3 does.** His name and his agreement appear in none of the 36 documents. Three protections stay, worded generally, because breaking them would cost JGN money whether or not he is named:

1. The brand is licensed, never sold (a sale makes the whole buyout due in 30 days).
2. No social account or handle moves to you or a company you control (not allowed except at fair market value).
3. Nosebleed Sports LLC collects its own subscription money in its own accounts (money JGN collects and passes on still counts toward his 20%).

**Risks of leaving him out.**

1. **It is not illegal and it is not hiding anything.** The binder is the company's own record book, not a disclosure to a buyer. Nothing in it is false without him.
2. **You must still disclose him when a buyer asks.** Any buyer's lawyer will ask who has owned JGN and whether any agreement restricts the brand. Answer truthfully and hand over the redemption agreement then. Leaving him out of the binder is fine; denying him in a sale is not, and a false statement in a purchase agreement is what creates liability.
3. **You lose one helpful sentence.** The binder no longer recites that he confirmed he has no claim to Nosebleed Sports LLC. That confirmation is still in the agreement he signed. Keep a signed copy with the record book so you can produce it in minutes.
4. **JGN's statements now carry the weight.** JGN states that no former member has any right in the code or the brand and that this deal breaks none of its agreements. You told me he contributed nothing to the platform, so the first is true; the second is true as long as the three protections above are followed.
5. **A buyer who finds out late trusts you less.** The fix is simple: put the redemption agreement in the data room on day one of any sale process.

Also from that agreement: JGN owes a $45,000 loan to a family member of Gabriel. It is JGN's debt, not the company's. A buyer's lawyer will still ask because the brand sits in JGN.

The company can defer payment until it has the cash, but the debt stands. If you want the same fair-value rule for the four of you, tell me and I make it uniform.
1. **Brock and the Operating Agreement (a risk to know, not a decision).** His signature on the amendment binds him to the Operating Agreement, which sets the terms that matter most to him: his units do not vote, your $12,990 comes back before he sees anything, the company can buy his units at fair value if he quits or is terminated for cause, and he can be dragged into a sale. If he signs without seeing it and later disputes one of those terms, his best argument is "I never saw it". The amendment answers that: it lists those terms in plain words in his acknowledgments and says a copy was his for the asking. The safest practice is still to hand it to him; this way is legally workable. He gets a copy within 10 days after closing either way, because a member is entitled to the company agreement.
2. **Which domain does the app run on?** Not needed to sign; needed for the account schedule after closing.
3. **Taxes.** Christian should file a protective 83(b) election within 30 days of closing and report about $3,227.33 of income (because his unvested units are bought back at fair value, they may count as his at closing in any case); Brock reports about $242.05. 4. **Paid picks and paid fantasy are regulated in some states.** The Operating Agreement requires a 70% vote and a written compliance plan before the company sells picks where a licence is needed, takes sportsbook or affiliate money, or runs fantasy leagues with entry fees or prizes. Worth one conversation with a gaming lawyer before launch.
5. **The fantasy non-compete is weaker until fantasy exists.** Courts enforce a non-compete to protect a business the company actually has. Until Nosebleed Sports Fantasy is being built, a founder who leaves could argue the fantasy part protects nothing. The paid-picks part is unaffected. The fantasy part also binds JGN while any of the four of you is restricted, so JGN cannot launch its own fantasy product in that time.
6. **Brock's podcast contract.** His talent agreement gives JGN the work product that relates to the brand and the social accounts. The podcast contract should say plainly that the podcast and its recordings belong to the company, so there is no overlap.

## 5. What the last stress test changed (confirm or flip)

A reviewer traced 13 situations through the documents (a founder quits, is removed, dies, is disabled; a sale; a failed closing; a dispute over the $12,990; a transfer; a new partner; JGN stops funding; Brock is terminated; a Glover claim; a founder who refuses to vote) and read them as each side's lawyer would. I fixed the drafting defects. Five of the fixes are choices you should know about:

1. **Votes about one person: any three of the other four.** The last build said 70% of the other founders' units. For a vote about a 13% holder that needed 4,620,000 units, and you plus two 13% holders have 4,600,000, so both you and Christian had to say yes every time. It is now 65% with at least three people, which is exactly "any three of the other four" in every case (math in the guide). It cuts both ways: Gabriel, Stephen and Michael together could find cause against you or against Christian if the facts fit the definition of cause.
2. **The sale protection is tighter.** After a sale, a buyer can only call it "cause" after written notice and 30 days to fix it; disputed money stays in escrow; the held-back money sits with an independent escrow agent; nothing can be set off against it. I also added death or disability after a sale to the events that pay a founder out in full.
3. **If you cannot act, Gabriel acts as CEO, then Christian** (signing, the bank, tax representative) until the founders vote in a successor, and Stephen, then Michael, acts as CTO if Christian cannot. Without this, your death or incapacity left no one able to sign or reach the bank account. (Updated in v3 to your order.)
4. **Future JGN money is credited equally to the four of you unless all four agree otherwise.** Before, JGN (meaning you, signing alone) could re-split it. It also only counts as capital if it paid an approved company expense and was reported to the founders.
5. **Brock's amendment takes effect on closing day, not when he signs.** If closing never happens, his agreement stays as it is today. A copy of the Operating Agreement is available to him on request before he signs, and he gets one within 10 days after closing.

Smaller fixes, no decision needed: a leaver's buyback price can no longer wipe out capital he was not paid for; unpaid buyback money is paid when the company has cash; "termination date" is defined; 350,000 reserve units are set aside for Brock's milestones; a quarterly budget approved by 70% vote; handles removed from the brand definition; JGN holds any stray platform money in trust for the company; JGN's notice to stop funding goes to every founder; a death leaves 90 days (not 30) to decide what happens to unvested units.

## 6. Risk that drafting cannot remove

1. **JGN's creditors, including Glover.** JGN hands its code to the company and receives nothing itself (its four owners get the units). While JGN owes Glover $12,600 and the $45,000 family loan, a creditor who went unpaid could attack that transfer. The JGN Agreement has JGN state that it is not doing this to avoid any creditor and can still pay its debts. That statement has to be true when you sign. The real protection is paying Glover on schedule; the best is a short written acknowledgment from him, or paying him off before a sale.
2. **The $12,990 valuation.** Christian's and Brock's tax on their units is based on the company being worth $12,990 on closing day. The memo values it at cost and gives no separate value to the brand license, the free promotion or the Discord members. It is signed by the founders, not an appraiser. If the IRS valued the company higher, their taxable amount would be higher. Put this to a CPA before signing.
3. **Leaving before a sale.** A founder who dies, is disabled or resigns for good reason before any sale keeps vested units but loses the unvested ones, the same as someone who quits. You asked for full payout when a buyer fires someone; you did not ask for it here, so I did not add it. Say so if you want it.
4. **Brock's percentages float.** His agreement measures each milestone percentage against the fully diluted units on the day it is issued. If the company ever authorizes more than 10,000,000 units, his milestone units scale up with it. His agreement also lets revenue received in the 60 days after termination count toward a milestone.
5. **Documents a buyer will ask for that are not in the binder.** Brock's talent agreement, the Glover redemption agreement and JGN's own operating agreement. The binder refers to all three. Keep signed copies with the record book.
6. **If you are out but not formally "unable to act".** Your consent right (Section 5.7) and your 2,000,000 votes stay with you while you are in service. If you are hospitalized or unreachable and the other founders have not yet confirmed in writing that you cannot act, nobody can give your consent and nobody can vote your units, so a sale, new units or an amendment would wait, and every 70% vote would need all four of the others. Gabe takes over day-to-day authority only once the confirmation or a disability finding is made. **Closed in v3:** document 36 names Gabe as your proxy and attorney-in-fact for exactly this (see Part 0).
