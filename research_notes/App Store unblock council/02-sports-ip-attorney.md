# Sports IP Attorney — What a zero-license US scores/odds/picks app can lawfully display

Persona: Sports IP attorney on the App Store unblock council. Scope: US law primary; UK/EU only where it bears on EPL / La Liga / Serie A / F1 data and marks. Everything under "Inferences" is my analysis, not a holding. Risk estimates are judgment calls, labelled as such. This is research for the council, not a legal opinion; a licensed attorney should review before anything is relied on.

Reading guide for the council: the single most important finding is that the facts (scores, schedules, odds, player names/stats) are safe; the marks (team/league names) are safe when used nominatively in a plain data UI; the logos/crests are not safe and no realistic fair-use theory covers them; and the BetUS sponsorship is the element that converts several "gray" items into "red" and is the one thing a league or a state regulator would actually act on.

---

## Key question 1 — Facts doctrine: are live scores and game facts free to display (NBA v. Motorola), and has hot-news / misappropriation narrowed that?

### Takeaway
Live scores, schedules, standings, box-score statistics and odds are uncopyrightable facts under US law; NBA v. Motorola remains good law and Barclays v. Theflyonthewall (2d Cir. 2011) narrowed the surviving hot-news exception further rather than reviving it. The UK/EU database right is the only serious "facts" doctrine that protects live football data, and it is a UK/EU-forum problem that a US-only app largely avoids but cannot completely ignore if it targets UK/EU users.

### Cited Findings
- NBA v. Motorola, 105 F.3d 841 (2d Cir. 1997): the Second Circuit held that transmission of "real-time" NBA game scores and information tabulated from television and radio broadcasts of games in progress does not constitute misappropriation of "hot news" that is the property of the NBA; the underlying games are not copyrightable although broadcasts of them are; only a narrow hot-news exception survives Copyright Act preemption — [Justia opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/105/841/598844/); [Harvard Berkman copy](https://cyber.harvard.edu/metaschool/fisher/linking/doctrine/nba.html)
- The surviving NBA hot-news test (as commentators summarise it) requires that plaintiff generate or gather information at a cost, the information be time-sensitive, defendant's use constitute free-riding, defendant be in direct competition, and the free-riding reduce the incentive to produce the information such that its existence is threatened — [Federalist Society, "The Hot News Doctrine Is Hot Again — Or Is It?"](https://fedsoc.org/publications/detail/hot-news-the-hot-news-doctrine-is-hot-again-or-is-it)
- Barclays Capital v. Theflyonthewall.com, 650 F.3d 876 (2d Cir. 2011): the Second Circuit reversed a hot-news injunction, holding the claim preempted by the Copyright Act because it fell within copyright's general scope and the conduct did not fit the narrow NBA exception — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Capital_Inc._v._Theflyonthewall.com,_Inc.); [Digital Media Law Project](https://www.dmlp.org/node/5772); [Wiley client alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-1978)
- Commentary on Barclays: the decision "sends an important signal to future litigants: that the hot news doctrine is today an unviable stand-alone claim in all but a very few situations" — [Wiley alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-1978)
- Morris Communications v. PGA Tour, 364 F.3d 1288 (11th Cir. 2004): the PGA Tour prevailed on antitrust grounds (valid business justification in preventing "free-riding" on its Real-Time Scoring System), not on a property right in scores; the court's footnote states the Tour's "property right does not come from copyright law, as copyright law does not protect factual information, like golf scores" — [OpenJurist opinion](https://m.openjurist.org/364/f3d/1288); [Legal Sports Report analysis](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/19654/pga-tour-court-victory-sports-data/)
- Major news organisations have told courts there is "no property right in the facts of a sporting event" — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/19689/property-rights-sporting-events/)
- UK/EU: Football Dataco v. Sportradar — the English Court of Appeal ([2013] EWCA Civ 27) held that the sui generis database right can subsist in "live football data" (the leagues' Football Live database of goals, cards, etc.) and that a foreign website operator can be liable in the UK where the inevitable consequence of UK access is infringement; the CJEU ruled UK courts have jurisdiction where the overseas party intends to target UK users — [EWCA Civ 2011/330](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2011/330); [EWHC 2012/1185](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2012/1185); [Fieldfisher summary](https://fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/live-football-data-may-be-protected-against-website-providers-that-make-users-infringe-in-the-uk)
- Football Dataco's exclusive rights in the leagues' live data passed to Genius Sports from May 2019 for five years; FDC says ~335 Sportradar-engaged scouts were ejected from stadia in 2018/19, and later claims named scouts as representative defendants — [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/football-dataco--or-genius-v-sportradar-latest-claims-bring-in-scouts-as-representative-defendants)
- Football Dataco v. Yahoo! (CJEU C-604/10, 1 Mar 2012): football fixture lists are not protected by database copyright because their compilation is dictated by rules leaving no room for creative freedom; "skill and labour ... even if significant, is not sufficient as such to trigger copyright protection" (para 42); the sui generis right had already been rejected by the national court — [SCL summary](https://www.scl.org/2407-football-dataco-no-database-copyright-protection-for-fixture-lists/); [CMS Law note](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/football-fixture-lists-has-the-final-whistle-been-blown-for-database-copyright)

### Inferences
- For a US-hosted app serving US users, the US rule controls: displaying live scores, period/inning state, schedules, standings, box scores and odds is GREEN. There is no US database right; Feist's facts/expression distinction plus NBA v. Motorola and Barclays make a hot-news claim by NFL/NBA/MLB/NHL/PGA Tour against a free consumer scores app close to non-viable (the app is not in direct competition with the league's data-production business and does not threaten the existence of the underlying information).
- The one US doctrine to watch is contract/access (Morris): a league can control real-time data only by controlling the pipe (credentialed access, API terms), not by owning the facts. That is why the ESPN-feed question (Key question 5) matters more than any "facts" theory.
- EPL/La Liga/Serie A: the UK database right (and its EU equivalents) protects the leagues' own live-data compilations, but only against extraction/re-utilisation of *that* database. An app that gets its football data from ESPN (which has its own licensed/independent feed) is not extracting from Football Live/Genius. The jurisdictional hook (Sportradar) requires intent to target UK/EU users. A US-only app with US-only marketing is low-exposure; geo-blocking the UK/EU, or at least not marketing there, is a cheap belt-and-braces measure. Fixture lists are free everywhere (Yahoo).
- Likelihood of a league C&D on *facts alone* (no logos, no sponsorship): very low (<5% per year, my estimate). Likely remedy if one came: a demand letter; an actual suit on facts would face Motorola/Barclays and is improbable.

### Gaps
- I did not find any post-2011 US decision reviving hot-news against a sports scores app; absence of cases is itself the signal, but I did not run a comprehensive docket search.
- Current Genius/Football Dataco enforcement posture (2024–2026) toward *consumer* scores apps (as opposed to bookmaker data suppliers) was not found; the documented targets are Sportradar and its scouts.

## Key question 2 — Nominative fair use of team and league names ("NFL", "Dallas Cowboys", "DAL") in a scores UI

### Takeaway
Plain-text team and league names and abbreviations used to identify which game and which teams a score refers to are classic nominative fair use under the Ninth Circuit's New Kids/Tabari test and the Third Circuit's Lendingtree variant; risk begins when the marks are used as the app's own branding, with league styling, or alongside a sportsbook sponsor in a way that implies endorsement.

### Cited Findings
- New Kids on the Block v. News America Publishing, 971 F.2d 302, 308 (9th Cir. 1992): a nominative use is permissible if (1) the product or service is not readily identifiable without use of the mark, (2) only so much of the mark is used as is reasonably necessary, and (3) the user does nothing that would, in conjunction with the mark, suggest sponsorship or endorsement by the holder — [CCB Journal, "A Beach Boy, A Playmate And Five Guys From Boston"](https://ccbjournal.com/articles/beach-boy-playmate-and-five-guys-boston-walk-courtroom-nominative-fair-use-trademarks); [Arnold & Porter on the circuit split](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2016/11/2016_11_16_second_circuit_expands_split__13324)
- A law review on the four-way circuit split notes that nominative fair use is what lets media name sports teams without infringement liability in most cases — [NC JOLT, "Is This Going to Be on the Test?"](https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/?p=4816)
- Toyota Motor Sales v. Tabari, 610 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2010): independent Lexus brokers' use of "lexus" in domain names was nominative fair use; the injunction was overbroad; in the Ninth Circuit the New Kids analysis replaces the ordinary likelihood-of-confusion test — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=9946); [CircleID](https://circleid.com/posts/20100716_ninth_circuit_opinion_on_domain_names_nominative_use_toyota_tabari)
- Century 21 Real Estate v. Lendingtree, 425 F.3d 211 (3d Cir. 2005): the Third Circuit adopted a two-step burden-shifting version — plaintiff first proves likelihood of confusion (using a modified set of Lapp factors), then defendant bears the burden of a three-prong nominative fair use defense (necessity of use, only so much as necessary, accurate reflection of the true relationship) — [Third Circuit opinion PDF](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/034700p5.pdf); [CCB Journal summary](https://ccbjournal.com/articles/nominative-use-mark-allowed); [Stanford CIS note](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2005/11/packets003558-shtml)
- The Second Circuit (Int'l Info. Sys. Sec. Cert. Consortium v. Security University, 2016) folds the nominative factors into the Polaroid confusion analysis rather than treating them as a standalone defense — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2016/11/2016_11_16_second_circuit_expands_split__13324)
- Apple's own guideline 5.2.1 bars using "protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission," and 4.1(c) bars using "another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple reviewers have rejected sports apps under 5.2.1 and, in a reported 2025-era case, under 4.1 for content that "resembles one or multiple third-party sports teams and/or leagues," asking for documentary evidence of rights — [Apple forum thread 718427](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/718427); [NBA content thread 130430](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/130430); [FIFA thread 96612](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/96612); [Reddit mirror of a 4.1 rejection, low-confidence source](https://r.datuan.dev/r/appledevelopers/duplicates/1uvcz63)

### Inferences
- GREEN: "NFL", "NBA", "Premier League", "Dallas Cowboys", "DAL", "Man City", "Scottie Scheffler", "Max Verstappen", "UFC 300" rendered in the app's own typeface inside a scoreboard/odds table. All three New Kids prongs are met: you cannot report a Cowboys score without saying "Cowboys" (or an abbreviation); plain text is the minimum use; a data grid implies nothing about endorsement. This is exactly the newspaper-sports-page use the NC JOLT article references. This holds in every circuit regardless of which version of the test applies, because the use is descriptive and non-stylised.
- GRAY (moves toward red as you add factors): (a) using the official league/team wordmark *stylisation* or fonts (that is use of more than necessary); (b) putting "NFL" or a team name in the app's name, icon, App Store title/subtitle or marketing ("Nosebleed NFL Scores") — this is app branding, violates Apple 4.1(c)/5.2.1 and the third New Kids prong; (c) team names in push-notification marketing copy that reads like a league product ("Your official Cowboys gameday alert"); (d) team names placed in the same visual unit as the BetUS logo ("Cowboys -3.5 at BetUS") — the sponsor's mark next to the league's mark is the classic implied-endorsement fact pattern (see Key question 7).
- A short, plain, visible disclaimer ("Not affiliated with or endorsed by any league, team or player; team names are used for identification only") supports the third prong and costs nothing. It does not cure logo use.
- Likelihood of a C&D over plain-text names in a data UI: very low (<5%/yr). Likelihood rises materially (my estimate 15–30%/yr for the most aggressive rightsholders — F1, Premier League, NCAA) if the name appears in the app name/icon or in paid promotion tied to a sportsbook.

### Gaps
- No reported US decision applying nominative fair use specifically to a live-scores app was found; the doctrine is applied by analogy (media reporting, Tabari, Lendingtree).
- Apple's practical tolerance for team *names* (as opposed to logos) in a scores app appears to be high (ESPN competitors exist with names only), but there is no written Apple statement to that effect; forum reports show reviewers sometimes still reject even after logo removal and ask for a note explaining the data rights — [thread 107074](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/107074).

## Key question 3 — Logos and crests: why they are off the table; is there any fair-use argument for tiny logos; who is licensed vs who relies on fair use; enforcement history

### Takeaway
Team logos and league shields are both registered trademarks and (for anything beyond simple lettering) copyrighted artwork; nominative fair use does not extend to using a logo when a name would do, Apple will not accept them without permission, and the only "fair use" defence (copyright § 107) is weak for a commercial, sponsor-supported product that uses the whole logo for its ordinary identifying purpose. No documented case of a league suing a scores app over logos was found, but Apple is the de facto enforcer and does not need a lawsuit.

### Cited Findings
- New Kids' second prong: "only so much of the mark ... as is reasonably necessary" — using a logo or distinctive lettering where a word suffices is the textbook example of exceeding what is necessary (the New Kids court contrasted using the words with using "distinctive lettering") — [CCB Journal summary of New Kids](https://ccbjournal.com/articles/beach-boy-playmate-and-five-guys-boston-walk-courtroom-nominative-fair-use-trademarks)
- Apple forum: a developer whose football app was rejected under 5.2.1 removed logos and renamed the app; Apple staff told him to read his stats-API provider's terms, which cover "Data/Logos/images" — [thread 718427](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/718427). Another developer removed logos and was still rejected; advice was to resubmit "without the logos (not even the small logos)" and leave a note on data permissions — [thread 107074](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/107074). Forum users note league logos are "most likely copyrighted not trademarked so their use is more restricted. You would need permission" — [thread 130430](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/130430) (user opinion, not Apple policy)
- Apple's 5.2.2 separately requires that if an app "uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service," the developer must be "specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- NFL enforcement pattern: the NFL and other leagues sent cease-and-desist letters to a seller placing team logos on shirts and, when he did not comply, had his website disabled; a court dismissed his suit against the leagues — [Foley Hoag, "Sue-per Bowl Shuffle II"](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/making-your-mark-blog/2016/february/sue-per-bowl-shuffle-ii-the-year-in-nfl-related-intellectual-property-litigation/). In 2023 the NFL sent C&Ds to New Orleans businesses over fleur-de-lis imagery close to the Saints' logo — [WLOX report](https://www.wlox.com/2023/07/22/one-new-orleans-company-fights-another-adjusts-after-nfl-cease-and-desist-letters)
- Licensed-competitor evidence: theScore Bet obtained an NFL partner designation in Ontario/Alberta that grants "access to NFL trademarks and league intellectual property, including NFL, Super Bowl, NFL Kickoff, and team marks" for marketing — i.e., logo rights are a negotiated, paid, geographically limited licence — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=280178); [SCCG](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/10/05/thescore-bet-obtains-official-nfl-partner-designation-for-ontario-and-alberta-operations/)
- Sportradar's NFL agreements cover official play-by-play and Next Gen Stats data distribution, not logos; Genius Sports later won the betting-data rights, with Sportradar reportedly remaining the media distributor — [Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/markets/north-america/46197-sportradar-named-exclusive-nfl-league-data-distributor/); [Sportsnaut on Genius](https://sportsnaut.com/nfl-genius-sports-reach-betting-data-deal/); [SBC News](https://www.sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2019/08/13/sportradar-data-clean-sweep-nfl-deal/)
- Manchester United v. Sega/Sports Interactive (High Court, 2020): the club sued Football Manager's makers over use of the club name and a generic substitute crest; the club's own lawyer called the crest argument "somewhat novel"; the court later refused to add a claim about third-party crest patches — [SI report](https://www.si.com/soccer/2020/05/22/manchester-united-sues-football-manager-video-game); [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/judge-rejects-man-utd-s-football-manager-fan-site-claims-19746)
- Premier League trademark posture: UKIPO revoked some Premier League name/logo registrations for certain goods/services in 2019 while the league won a partial opposition in gaming classes; the league has filed US marks covering NFTs/virtual goods and the FA has filed for "Fantasy Premier League" — [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/premier-league-in-score-draw-with-gaming-companies-over-tms-17764); [Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/premier-league-web3-metaverse-trademarks/)

### Inferences
- RED: hotlinked or self-hosted team logos, league shields, club crests, F1 team liveries/logos, UFC logo, PGA Tour "swinging golfer", tour/league wordmarks in official styling. Reasons: (1) trademark — the logo is more than necessary (New Kids prong 2 fails), and a sponsor-supported app is commercial use; (2) copyright — crests and shields are pictorial works; a § 107 defence fails on factors 1 (commercial, non-transformative: the logo is used for exactly its identifying purpose), 3 (entire work), and 4 (the leagues run a licensing market for exactly this use, see theScore's paid deal); (3) Apple 5.2.1 — Apple rejects on sight regardless of the legal merits, and the forum threads show reviewers also look at "small" logos.
- The "tiny logos in a grid like ESPN/Yahoo do it" argument does not work: ESPN/Yahoo/theScore are licensed (ESPN is a league broadcaster; theScore holds an NFL partner licence in Canada). Sofascore/Flashscore/365Scores are EU-based, operate under different risk calculus, and I found no evidence either way on whether their logo use is licensed (see Gaps). "Others do it" is not a defence and Apple's reviewer is the gate anyway.
- Likelihood of consequences for logos: Apple rejection ~certain (already happened). League C&D if the logos ship on the web app: moderate (my estimate 10–25%/yr across 12+ rightsholders; higher once a sportsbook sponsor is on the page). Likely remedy: demand to remove; damages exposure if sued is statutory copyright damages per work (up to $150,000 per work if wilful) plus Lanham Act profits — a scores app's profits are small, but 100+ logos × statutory damages is a real number and the NFL has used DMCA/host takedowns rather than litigation.
- The team-name + abbreviation + colour fallback is the correct design direction; see Key question 4 for the colour caveat.

### Gaps
- No documented C&D or lawsuit by any US league against a *scores/odds app* specifically over hotlinked logos was found (searches on NFL/NBA/MLB/NHL/UFC/PGA app enforcement returned apparel, streaming-piracy and video-game cases). This is an absence-of-evidence finding, not evidence that it is safe. A satirical Onion result ("NFL sues EA") appeared in search and must be ignored.
- Licensing status of Sofascore, Flashscore, 365Scores, Action Network logo displays could not be verified; only Sofascore's Saudi Pro League and Flashscore's non-league club deals were found — [Sofascore news](https://www.sofascore.com/es-la/news/sofascore-partners-with-the-saudi-pro-league-new-highlights-and-livestream-options); [Walton & Hersham FC](https://waltonhershamfc.com/?p=10974).

## Key question 4 — Team colours: protectable trade dress? Does a coloured circle with "DAL" in navy/silver cross the line?

### Takeaway
Colour schemes can be protected as trade dress once they acquire secondary meaning (Qualitex; Smack Apparel), but Smack Apparel turned on colours *plus* other school-referencing indicia on merchandise sold to fans; using team colours as a background for a nominatively-fair-use abbreviation inside a data UI is materially different and is gray-leaning-green, provided the colour treatment is not shaped into a logo-like badge.

### Cited Findings
- Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products, 514 U.S. 159 (1995): colour alone can be a trademark where it has acquired secondary meaning and is not functional — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/159/)
- Wal-Mart Stores v. Samara Bros., 529 U.S. 205 (2000): product-design trade dress (and, per Qualitex, colour) is never inherently distinctive; secondary meaning is always required — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/529/205/)
- Board of Supervisors for LSU v. Smack Apparel, 550 F.3d 465 (5th Cir. 2008): the Fifth Circuit affirmed that the universities' colour schemes, though not inherently distinctive, had acquired secondary meaning, and held that "the colors, content, and context" of Smack's t-shirts were likely to cause confusion as to source, sponsorship or affiliation; the district court had also found non-functionality and rejected laches because the copying was intentional — [Fifth Circuit opinion PDF](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions%5Cpub%5C07/07-30580-CV0.wpd.pdf); [World Trademark Review](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/fifth-circuit-applies-trademark-protection-universities-colour-schemes); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/schools-can-protect-their-team-colors/); [Foley summary](https://www.foley.com/?p=58004)
- Secondary sources on sports trademark law treat Smack Apparel as the leading authority that team colours in combination with other indicia are protectable — [LexPlug sports-law outline](https://www.lexplug.com/outlines/sports-law/protection-of-team-names-logos-and-colors)

### Inferences
- The holding in Smack Apparel is narrower than "team colours are owned": the shirts used the colour schemes together with slogans and references (game scores, "Sugar Bowl" style phrases, city names) that were designed to call the schools to mind, and were sold into the same merchandise market the schools license. Colour was one element of a confusion finding on apparel.
- A scores app row that renders "DAL" on a navy chip and "PHI" on a midnight-green chip is (a) not merchandise, (b) not sold in the leagues' licensing market, (c) uses colour as a UI affordance to distinguish two rows of data, and (d) pairs the colour with a nominatively-fair-use abbreviation. On Smack's own "colors, content and context" framing, the context is a data table, not a fan product. I classify this GRAY-GREEN: a league would have to prove consumers think the *app* is league-sponsored because its rows are tinted, which is a hard case for them.
- Where it tips to GRAY-RED: rendering the colours as a shield/circle/badge shape with the abbreviation in a bold condensed sans that mimics the team's wordmark, i.e. designing a faux-logo. The more the chip reads as an emblem, the closer it gets to a stylised mark (New Kids prong 2) and to a colourable copyright claim if it evokes the actual crest. Keep the shape generic (square/rounded rectangle/thin left border), the type the app's own font, and the colour used consistently as a system-wide data-visualisation convention.
- Collegiate note: NCAA schools and the Collegiate Licensing Company were the plaintiffs in Smack Apparel; college colour-scheme claims are the best-established, so the NCAA football/basketball views deserve the most conservative colour treatment.
- Likelihood of a colour-based C&D on a data chip: low (<5%/yr); likelihood of a league *adding* a colour-scheme count to a complaint that is really about logos or sponsorship: moderate. The colour chip is not what would trigger a letter, but it will be pleaded if a letter comes for another reason.

### Gaps
- No case applying colour-scheme trade dress to a software UI or scores app was found; the analysis is by analogy from merchandise cases. The Fifth Circuit opinion PDF could not be fetched through the proxy, so the quotations above are from the court's URL as cited by secondary sources and should be checked against the opinion text (550 F.3d 465, 2008).

## Key question 5 — Hotlinking ESPN CDN images and proxying undocumented ESPN JSON: copyright/ToS/CFAA exposure to ESPN/Disney

### Takeaway
The ESPN exposure is separate from, and in practice more likely to bite than, the league exposure: hotlinking ESPN-hosted logos/headshots raises a display-right question that splits the circuits (server test), and server-side proxying of ESPN's undocumented JSON is almost certainly a breach of Disney's Terms of Use even though it is probably not a CFAA violation after hiQ/Van Buren. Apple guideline 5.2.2 independently requires proof that ESPN's terms permit the use, which they do not.

### Cited Findings
- Disney's Terms of Use (which govern ESPN sites) bar using robots, spiders, scrapers or other automated or manual processes to access, monitor or copy any part of the Disney services without written permission — [Disney GTOU, Spanish-language PDF of the 2016 terms](https://disneytermsofuse.com/app/uploads/2020/09/disney_gtou_20160331v2_Spanish-Latin-American-TOU.pdf) (the English page at disneytermsofuse.com could not be fetched through the research proxy; a third-party tracker excerpts a current clause barring automated access "including for purposes of creating or developing any AI tool, data mining, or web scraping" — [Conduct Atlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/disney/disney-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-022520/prohibition-on-automated-access-and-ai-scraping/), unverified)
- Disney states it does not permit anyone to "access, copy, extract, or use Disney content for text and data mining" and has opted out of EU TDM exceptions — [Disney AI opt-out page](https://www.thelionking.co.uk/ai-opt-out)
- Disney has sent C&Ds to scrapers before (a dining-reservation site received a letter alleging copyright, trademark and DMCA violations) — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/?p=73177); ESPN itself was sued by a sports data provider alleging CFAA-based scraping — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/mining-of-baseball-data-yields-litigation) (details not verified)
- The ESPN JSON endpoints are undocumented, require no key, and third-party guides warn that heavy automated use may violate ESPN's terms and that ESPN can change or remove them at any time — [Zuplo guide](https://zuplo.com/blog/2024/10/01/espn-hidden-api-guide); [sportsapis.dev](https://sportsapis.dev/espn-api)
- hiQ v. LinkedIn (9th Cir. 2022, on remand after Van Buren): "the concept of 'without authorization' does not apply to public websites"; CFAA liability attaches to circumventing generally applicable access gates such as passwords; non-CFAA claims (breach of contract, trespass to chattels, copyright, misappropriation) remain available — [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=23817); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/taking-cue-supreme-court-s-van-buren-decision-ninth-circuit-releases-new-opinion); [Farella Braun](https://www.fbm.com/publications/hiqs-groundbreaking-injunction-against-linkedin-reaffirmed-scraping-of-publicly-available-data-likely-does-not-violate-cfaa/)
- Ryanair v. Booking.com (D. Del., jury verdict July 2024): jury found Booking.com violated the CFAA with intent to defraud, after Ryanair deployed a blocking system ("Shield") and sent C&Ds that were circumvented; damages were $5,000 — [Ryanair press release](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-wins-its-case-against-travel-giant-booking-com-in-delaware-court-ruling/?market=it); [H&H Law analysis](https://hh-law.com/blogs/litigation-blogs-latest-legal-law-news-and-cases/ryanair-jury-verdict-against-booking-com-cfaa/)
- Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. 2024): summary judgment for the scraper; Meta's terms reached only logged-in users, so logged-out scraping of public data was not a breach; Meta then dismissed its remaining claim. X Corp. v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. May 2024): contract claim dismissed for failure to allege damage; claims based on copying public data were preempted by the Copyright Act — [Proskauer New Media Law](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2024/05/14/california-court-issues-another-noteworthy-decision-dismissing-breach-of-contract-and-tort-claims-in-web-scraping-dispute/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com:443/publications/article/395944/judge-throws-out-xs-claims-against-data-scraper.html) (note: Proskauer represented Bright Data)
- Server test: Hunley v. Instagram (9th Cir. July 2023) reaffirmed Perfect 10's server test — a site that embeds an image hosted elsewhere does not "display" it; S.D.N.Y. courts (Goldman v. Breitbart, 302 F. Supp. 3d 585 (2018); Nicklen v. Sinclair; McGucken v. Newsweek) have rejected the test and treat embedding as a display — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2023/07/embedded-instagram-content-passes-the-server); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102ille/the-server-test-survives-in-the-ninth-circuit-at-least-for-now)
- Apple 5.2.2: "If your app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Hotlinking ESPN-hosted logos/headshots (client-side `<img src="a.espncdn.com/...">`): copyright exposure to ESPN/Getty/AP as to the *photographs* (headshots are copyrighted photos; team logos are the leagues', not ESPN's). Under the Ninth Circuit server test the app does not "display"; under the S.D.N.Y. line it does. Disney is a New York/California litigant and would pick the forum. Independently it is a breach of Disney's ToS (automated copying/use of content) and, for the logos, the league problem in Key question 3 is unchanged — hotlinking does not launder the trademark use. Classification: RED for logos regardless of host; RED-GRAY for headshots (see Key question 7); the hosting method does not matter to Apple.
- Server-side proxying of ESPN's undocumented JSON: CFAA — likely not actionable while the endpoints are public and un-gated (hiQ), but if ESPN blocks the proxy's IPs or sends a C&D and the app circumvents (rotating IPs, spoofed headers), Ryanair shows a jury will find CFAA "intent to defraud" on those facts. Breach of contract — Disney's ToS plainly prohibit automated copying; the Bright Data cases show the weak points for Disney are (a) whether a server-to-server fetch with no account ever assented to the ToS (browsewrap problem) and (b) proving damages; but a hosted, Apple-reviewed commercial app with a sponsor is a far less sympathetic defendant than Bright Data, and Disney's remedy of choice is simply to block or change the endpoint and to tell Apple. Trespass to chattels requires actual impairment of ESPN's servers (Intel v. Hamidi line) — negligible for a cached, low-volume proxy. Classification: GRAY for the web app; RED for the iOS submission because 5.2.2 requires written permission "upon request" that does not exist.
- Practical likelihood: ESPN changing or rate-limiting the endpoints (not a legal act) is the most probable failure mode and is close to certain over a multi-year horizon. A Disney C&D is low-moderate (5–15%/yr) at small scale and rises with traffic and with the sponsor banner. A lawsuit is unlikely unless the app ignores a letter.
- Mitigation path that changes the classification: replace ESPN JSON with a licensed data API (the Odds API already covers odds; a licensed scores/schedule feed such as Sportradar, Genius, SportsDataIO, API-Sports, etc.) whose terms expressly grant display rights — that is also the single document Apple's reviewer asks for under 5.2.2 and in [thread 718427](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/718427).

### Gaps
- The current English text of the Disney Terms of Use could not be retrieved (DNS failure through the proxy); the clause cited is from the 2016 non-English PDF and a third-party tracker. Someone should pull https://disneytermsofuse.com/english/ directly and copy the "Robots, Spiders, Scrapers" and "Framing/Inline linking" sections.
- No reported case or public C&D involving ESPN's undocumented JSON endpoints specifically was found.

## Key question 6 — Odds display: sportsbook names/prices from The Odds API; state-law issues with showing offshore book odds and linking to offshore books

### Takeaway
Odds are facts and sportsbook names are nominative; the rights issue is contractual (The Odds API's terms) rather than IP. The serious problem is regulatory: displaying BetUS odds and deep-linking to BetUS with a paid sponsorship is "promoting" an unlicensed gambling business, which state regulators have treated since 2014 as actionable against affiliates, which licensed operators now police among their own affiliates, and which federal aiding-and-abetting/conspiracy theories under the Wire Act/UIGEA have been used to threaten since the 2000s.

### Cited Findings
- The Odds API publishes an integration guide offering a widget that "displays bookmaker odds with the site owner's affiliate links" and a raw API for custom displays; the public guide does not state licensing terms — [The Odds API guide](https://the-odds-api.com/guide/website-odds-integration.html). Its terms and conditions exist (tracked copy dated 7/11/2026) but the clause text was not retrievable — [ToS Tracker](https://tostracker.app/document/the-odds-apicom-tos). Comparable odds-data vendors condition display rights on attribution and prohibit redistribution of raw responses — [The Rundown licensing doc](https://docs.therundown.io/licensing-and-attribution); [odds-api.io terms](https://odds-api.io/terms)
- New Jersey DGE / AG (April 2014) sent cease-and-desist letters to six affiliate sites (PokerSource, RaketheRake, CardsChat, Pokersites, RakeBrain, others) for promoting unlicensed operators including Bovada, warning of "prosecution for offering Internet gaming without approval and/or promoting gambling"; DGE Advisory Bulletin 2015-01 (June 4, 2015) gave affiliates 150 days to stop and certify under oath — [Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/legal/19093-nj-regulator-targets-affiliates-promoting-illegal-gaming-sites/); [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/law-and-regulation/new-jersey-regulator-demands-removal-of-affiliate-links-to-unlicensed-online-poker-rooms-21-05/); [Klein Moynihan on the bulletin](https://kleinmoynihan.com/new-jersey-sets-compliance-deadline-for-internet-gaming-affiliates)
- 2025 state enforcement against the offshore books themselves (BetUS is named repeatedly): Michigan Gaming Control Board C&D to BetUS (May 2025, 14 days to comply, coordinated with the Michigan AG) — [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2025/05/08/104126-michigan-issues-ceaseanddesist-orders-to-offshore-gambling-site-betus); Florida Gaming Control Commission C&Ds to BetUS, Bovada and MyBookie before the Super Bowl — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/223576/cd-florida-sports-betting-mybookie-bovada-betus/); Massachusetts AG C&Ds to BetOnline and Sportsbetting.ag (June 2025) alleging marketing to residents "using U.S. teams, players, and content creators," with civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation — [Mass.gov press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/attorney-generals-office-issues-cease-and-desist-letters-to-two-online-gambling-operators-for-illegal-offshore-casino-and-sports-betting-operations); Minnesota AG orders to 14 sites (Nov 2025) — [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2025/11/07/116223-minnesota-attorney-general-issues-ceaseanddesist-orders-to-14-gambling-sites); Michigan MGCB to BetNow (Feb 2025) — [Michigan.gov](https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/news/2025/02/24/gaming-control-board-issues-cease-and-desist-letter-to-betnow); Bovada exits from several states after 2024 C&Ds — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/michigan-colorado-bovada-sports-betting-shut-down-june-2024); Michigan has considered targeting Bovada's *suppliers* — [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-michigan-considers-targeting-bovada-suppliers-more-offshore-gaming-sites)
- Tennessee Sports Wagering Council (June 2025) fined two offshore books $50,000 each and stated that "any businesses in Tennessee working with illegal offshore sportsbooks could face criminal penalties" — [Public Gaming](https://members.publicgaming.com/news-categories/regulatory-issues/14528-tennessee-sports-betting-regulator-fines-two-offshore-sportsbooks)
- 50 state attorneys general wrote to the US DOJ in 2025 asking for federal enforcement against unlicensed offshore gambling, naming Bovada, MyBookie and BetOnline as examples — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/personal-finance/americans-expected-bet-record-30-billion-2025-nfl-season-legally-rcna227989)
- FanDuel Group warned its partners and affiliates by email that it considers offshore sites illegal operations and may terminate affiliates that promote them — [GGB News](https://ggbnews.com/article/fanduel-group-warns-affiliates-on-offshore-gambling-sites) (undated)
- Federal theories: DOJ advised the National Association of Broadcasters that members risked aiding-and-abetting prosecution for running ads for online gambling operations; the Wire Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1084, reaches transmission of bets and of information assisting in placing bets on sporting events — [CRS, Internet Gambling: An Abridged Overview of Federal Criminal Law](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20120124_RS21984_f13cb1f61558b2318f016fd06123119d14fe617c.html). Practitioner commentary: aiding-and-abetting liability can be imposed on those who produce or run ads for illegal gambling operations if they know the site is illegal and provide substantial assistance; no federal statute expressly bans the advertising itself; affiliates could also be charged with conspiracy to violate UIGEA — [Ifrah Law, iGBNA](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/igbna_20_augsept_p12-13.pdf); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppardmullin.com/media/publication/56_pub608.pdf)
- Official-data mandates: Tennessee (2019) and Illinois (2019) required licensed operators to use official league data for in-play/prop bets on "commercially reasonable terms"; Tennessee later dropped the mandate (2023); the mandate spread to about seven states — [Legal Sports Report on Illinois](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/37240/illinois-sports-betting-rules-commercially-reasonable/); [Vixio on Tennessee](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-tennessee-drop-data-mandate-part-sports-wagering-reshuffle)
- Apple 5.3.4: apps that "offer real money gaming (such as sports betting ...) must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free"; 5.3: "Only include this functionality if you've fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere you make your app available" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Displaying odds (numbers) and the names of the books that offer them: GREEN on IP grounds (facts + nominative use of "DraftKings", "FanDuel", "BetMGM"). Official-data mandates bind licensed *operators*, not media; they do not restrict a scores app from showing odds. The only constraint is The Odds API's contract — pull the current terms, confirm the plan permits public commercial display and what attribution is required, and keep that document for Apple's 5.2.2 request.
- Displaying BetUS odds and deep-linking to BetUS, with BetUS paying the app: RED in the licensed states and gray-red everywhere else. The 2014 NJ affiliate letters are the direct precedent (promoting an unlicensed operator = "promoting gambling"); Tennessee's 2025 statement extends criminal-penalty warnings to "businesses working with" offshore books; Massachusetts' 2025 letters specifically attacked marketing "using U.S. teams, players, and content creators" — which describes this app's layout. State penal codes (e.g., NY Penal Law art. 225 "promoting gambling," Mass. G.L. c. 93A consumer protection as used against Bovada) are the vehicles; the typical remedy is a C&D and, for the operator, civil penalties per violation. Prosecution of a small affiliate is rare, but affiliate C&Ds are documented and licensed operators (FanDuel) now treat offshore promotion as grounds to cut affiliates off — relevant if the app ever wants a licensed sponsor.
- Federal exposure is lower-probability but higher-severity: the Wire Act's "information assisting in the placing of bets" language and aiding-and-abetting/conspiracy theories have been used as threats since 2003; I found no prosecution of a pure scores app, but the 2025 50-AG letter to DOJ is a signal that the offshore sector is the current enforcement target and BetUS is one of the named class.
- Apple: a scores app does not "offer real money gaming," so 5.3.4 does not apply on its face; but reviewers read links to an unlicensed sportsbook as facilitating illegal gambling and Apple's 5.3 preamble ("fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere") gives them cover to reject. Expect the BetUS link to be a second, independent rejection ground after logos.
- Practical rule I would give the council: show BetUS odds as one row of data only if the row is not paid and not linked; the moment there is a paid placement or an outbound link, geo-fence it out of every state that regulates sports betting (which by 2026 is most of the market) or drop it. "Show it everywhere and see" is not an option I can endorse.

### Gaps
- The Odds API's actual commercial-display and attribution clauses were not retrievable; someone must read https://the-odds-api.com/terms-and-conditions.html.
- I found no 2024–2026 regulator action aimed at an *affiliate or media site* (as opposed to the operator); the affiliate precedents are NJ 2014–15 and Tennessee's 2025 warning. That is a thin but real record; the trend line (operators, then suppliers, then affiliates) points the wrong way.
- No state-by-state table of "advertising unlicensed gambling" statutes was compiled; NY Penal Law art. 225 and Mass. c. 93A are cited from memory and should be confirmed.

## Key question 7 — Does the BetUS association increase trademark / right-of-publicity exposure? Player headshots in a betting-adjacent product

### Takeaway
Yes. Leagues enforce hardest where their marks sit next to gambling they have not blessed (NCAA v. DraftKings 2026; NFL's Approved Sportsbook Operator program), so a sponsor banner from an unlicensed book converts nominative team names from green to gray and makes any logo use a near-certain target. Player names and stats are protected speech (CBC, Daniels), but player *photographs* are third-party copyrights and their use in a sponsor-supported betting-adjacent product is the weakest spot after the logos.

### Cited Findings
- NCAA v. DraftKings (S.D. Ind., filed March 2026): the NCAA sued over DraftKings' use of "March Madness," pleading that it "has purposefully avoided any appearance of affiliation with gambling companies" and seeking a permanent injunction for false association — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/?p=325100); [Crowell & Moring, "NCAA's anti-sports-betting stance becomes an IP issue"](https://www.crowell.com/a/web/92DLZef8Ns1px7QygHECvy/ncaas-anti-sports-betting-stance-becomes-an-ip-issue.pdf)
- NFL: from 2020 teams may take sportsbook sponsors; by 2021 seven "Approved Sportsbook Operators" (BetMGM, FOX Bet, PointsBet, WynnBET, Caesars, DraftKings, FanDuel) had the right to use NFL brand assets in betting products — [SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/news/nfl-to-allow-sportsbook-sponsors-in-2020); [Public Gaming](https://publicgaming.com/news-categories/sports-betting-daily-fantasy-sports/5822-nfl-inches-toward-stadium-sports-betting-as-sportsbook-sponsors-permitted); theScore Bet's 2026 Canadian deal shows the same assets being licensed market-by-market — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=280178)
- Massachusetts AG's 2025 letters to BetOnline/Sportsbetting.ag specifically cited marketing "using U.S. teams, players, and content creators" — [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/news/attorney-generals-office-issues-cease-and-desist-letters-to-two-online-gambling-operators-for-illegal-offshore-casino-and-sports-betting-operations)
- C.B.C. Distribution & Marketing v. MLB Advanced Media, 505 F.3d 818 (8th Cir. 2007): fantasy operator's use of MLB player names and statistics without licence — the Eighth Circuit affirmed judgment for CBC; it accepted that the Missouri right-of-publicity elements (use of name as symbol of identity, without consent, for commercial advantage) were implicated but held the First Amendment interest in publicly available information about games supersedes the players' rights — [Eighth Circuit opinion PDF](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/07/10/063357P.pdf); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/fantasy-sports-operator-retains-winning-streak-over-mlb-division/). (A search-engine summary characterised the Eighth Circuit as ruling for MLBAM; that is wrong — the judgment for CBC was affirmed. Verify against the PDF.)
- Daniels v. FanDuel (Ind. 2018, on certified question from the 7th Cir., 884 F.3d 672): Indiana Supreme Court unanimously held that DFS operators' use of college players' names, pictures and statistics falls within the statute's "newsworthy value" exception; the Seventh Circuit framed the question as whether the name appears "in newsworthy material or in connection with reporting on an event" — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2018/11/daniels-v-fanduel); [Husch Blackwell](https://huschblackwell.com/inthenews/indiana-supreme-court-finds-exception-to-right-of-publicity-statute); [884 F.3d 672](https://cite.case.law/f3d/884/672/); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/25251/draftkings-fanduel-indiana-fanasy-sports-lawsuit/)
- O'Melveny's post-Murphy analysis frames CBC and Daniels as the governing authorities for athlete publicity rights in betting-adjacent products — [O'Melveny](https://www.omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/murphy-and-athletes-publicity-rights/)
- Server-test split on embedded photos (Hunley vs. Goldman/Nicklen/McGucken) — see Key question 5 — [DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit)

### Inferences
- Trademark: the third New Kids prong ("nothing that suggests sponsorship") is the one the BetUS banner attacks. A page that reads "NFL · Cowboys vs Eagles · presented by BetUS" lets a league plead false association under § 43(a) with a fact pattern it has a policy reason to litigate (NCAA v. DraftKings is the live example, and it involves a *licensed* operator; an unlicensed, state-C&D'd operator is a far better plaintiff story). Mitigation that keeps names green: physically and verbally separate the sponsor unit from team/league names; never put "NFL/NBA/..." inside the sponsored unit; no "official," "partner," "gameday" language; an explicit non-affiliation line in the sponsor unit.
- Right of publicity — names and stats: GREEN under CBC/Daniels, and those cases involved paid fantasy contests, which is a stronger commercial use than a scores page. Caveat: both are First Amendment/"newsworthy" holdings about *information*; they do not cover use of a player's name or likeness *in the advertisement itself* (e.g., "Bet on Dak with BetUS"). Keep player names out of any sponsored unit.
- Player headshots hotlinked from ESPN: RED-GRAY. The images are copyrighted (ESPN/Getty/AP/league), hotlinking only helps in the Ninth Circuit, Disney's ToS forbid it, and in a betting-adjacent product a plaintiff can add a publicity claim arguing the photo was used to sell the sponsor rather than to report. The clean route is names and initials, or licensed headshots from a data vendor whose terms grant display rights.
- The BetUS association also changes who is likely to write first. Without it, the realistic correspondent is Apple; with it, the realistic correspondents are (1) a state regulator (Michigan, Massachusetts, Tennessee, Florida and Minnesota have all acted in 2025 and name BetUS or its peers), (2) a league's outside counsel on the false-association theory, and (3) any future licensed sponsor's compliance team (FanDuel's affiliate warning). My estimate: with the BetUS placement live and geo-unrestricted, probability of *some* adverse letter within 12 months is moderate-to-high (30–50%); without it and with logos removed, low (<10%).

### Gaps
- NFL's written gambling/sponsorship policy text (as opposed to news reports of it) was not retrieved; I found no explicit NFL rule naming offshore operators, only the Approved Sportsbook Operator structure.
- No reported case of a league suing a *media* site over an offshore-book sponsorship was found; the NCAA v. DraftKings and 2010-era "Jacks or Better" casino-boat cases (Jaguars/NFL/Garrard suit over ads using a player photo — primary link not verified) are the closest analogues.
- Current MLB/NBA public statements on "official data" and on media use of data (2024–2026) were not found; the official-data mandates found are about licensed operators, not media.

## Key question 8 — Highest-aggression rightsholders: Formula One Licensing BV, Premier League, La Liga, Serie A clubs, UEFA, Olympics — enforcement patterns

### Takeaway
F1 and the Olympic movement are the two rightsholders most likely to object to *names and branding* (F1 polices the "F1" mark against creators and domains; the USOPC has a statutory monopoly that needs no confusion showing); the Premier League, La Liga and Serie A are overwhelmingly focused on stream piracy and app-store takedowns of IPTV apps, with the football database right as their data weapon; UFC/Zuffa's documented enforcement is piracy and video games, not scores apps.

### Cited Findings
- Formula One Licensing BV: repeated WIPO UDRP complaints over "F1" domains (e.g., thef1magazine.com, 2023) alleging attempts "to attract, for commercial gain, users ... by creating a likelihood of confusion with the F1 mark" — [WIPO D2023-4186](https://wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/pdf/2023/d2023-4186.pdf); [WIPO D2021-3215](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/text/2021/d2021-3215.html); [WIPO D2016-0121](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/text/2016/d2016-0121.html)
- F1 and Formula One World Championship Ltd filed a 2024 federal counterfeiting suit in Nevada, pleading over 150 US trademark registrations covering "F1" and "Formula 1" — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/f1-files-suit-over-counterfeits-amid-rise-in-u-s-demand)
- F1's lawyers reportedly sent cease-and-desist letters to content creators with "F1" in their handles, concentrating on creators using the mark commercially or implying official affiliation — [Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/?p=1074077); [Multiplayer.it (Italian)](https://multiplayer.it/notizie/formula-1-cambiare-nome-famosi-influencer.html)
- Limits on the F1 mark: a 2007 UK registry decision found "simply too much evidence that F1 is indeed a generic term" and refused an F1 registration for certain services; in 2020 the UKIPO declined to let Formula One block a third-party registration on confusion grounds — [GrandPrix.com (2007)](https://www.grandprix.com/news/formula-one-and-f1.html); [World IP Review, "Formula One stalls in the third"](https://www.worldipreview.com/trademark/formula-one-stalls-in-the-third)
- Premier League: 2019 season opened with 16 cease-and-desist letters aimed at pirate streams — [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/copyright/premier-league-season-opens-with-16-cease-and-desist-letters-18508); UKIPO partially revoked the league's name/logo marks in 2019 — [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/premier-league-in-score-draw-with-gaming-companies-over-tms-17764); the leagues' live-data rights are enforced through Football Dataco/Genius against Sportradar and its scouts — [Mishcon](https://www.mishcon.com/news/football-dataco--or-genius-v-sportradar-latest-claims-bring-in-scouts-as-representative-defendants)
- Manchester United v. Sega/SI over the club name and generic crest in Football Manager (2020) — [SI](https://www.si.com/soccer/2020/05/22/manchester-united-sues-football-manager-video-game); [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/judge-rejects-man-utd-s-football-manager-fan-site-claims-19746)
- La Liga: obtained removal of IPTV apps (Newplay, IPTV Smarters Pro) from Google/Apple/Huawei stores, reported results against 20 IPTV services and 30 streaming sites, and in 2024 accused the platforms of disobedience for not disabling installed apps; its dynamic IP blocking has caused collateral blocking of unrelated Cloudflare-hosted sites (reported Feb 2026) — [Genbeta](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/laliga-obliga-a-iptv-smarters-pro-a-desaparecer-google-play-store-prometen-que-atacaran-a-apps-iptv); [BandaAncha](https://bandaancha.eu/articulos/laliga-querellara-contra-google-espana-11093); [Xataka Móvil](https://www.xatakamovil.com/streaming/laliga-queria-borrar-apps-iptv-nuestros-moviles-ano-despues-sigue-conseguirlo); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/la-liga)
- Serie A sent Google a formal demand over the "Live Football" streaming app that kept reappearing under altered names and asked for developer identity data — [Calcio e Finanza](https://www.calcioefinanza.it/?p=168828)
- UFC/Zuffa: suits against Take-Two (post-licence sales) and Ubisoft ("Ultimate Fighting" packaging), court orders against stream pirates (HQ-Streams), domain seizures, and TROs against souvenir vendors — [Las Vegas Sun (Take-Two)](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2010/dec/10/ufc-sues-video-game/); [Las Vegas Sun (streams)](https://m.lasvegassun.com/news/2010/mar/15/ufc-sues-web-sites-promoting-broadcasts-fights/); [UFC.com on HQ-Streams](https://www.ufc.com/news/zuffa-erreicht-unterlassungserklarung-gegen-hq-streamscom-zur-wahrung-ihrer-urheberrechte); [Review-Journal](https://preview.reviewjournal.com/crime/courts/ufcs-lawyers-come-out-swinging/amp)
- Olympics: San Francisco Arts & Athletics v. USOC, 483 U.S. 522 (1987) upheld the Amateur Sports Act's grant to the USOC of exclusive rights in "Olympic" without a likelihood-of-confusion requirement — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/483/522/)

### Inferences
- F1: the word "F1"/"Formula 1" is the sport's name and nominative use of it to label a results page is defensible (and the UK registry's genericness findings help), but Formula One Licensing BV is the rightsholder most likely to send a letter over *branding* — any "F1" in the app name, handle, icon, notification title, or sponsored unit. Team names (Ferrari, Red Bull Racing, McLaren) are corporate marks with their own owners; plain-text use in a results table is nominative. Driver names and timing data are facts. Risk for plain-text F1 results: low; for "F1" in product branding or next to BetUS: moderate-high. Team/constructor logos: red, same as the leagues.
- Premier League / La Liga / Serie A: the app is not a stream, so their main enforcement machinery (piracy takedowns, IP blocking) does not aim at it; the two live risks are (1) crests (red, as above, and club-level — Man Utd's crest claim against Football Manager shows clubs will plead even against a generic substitute crest) and (2) the database right if the app targets UK/EU users with data traceable to Football Live/Genius (see Key question 1). Serie A's and La Liga's use of app-store takedown processes shows they are comfortable going through Apple/Google rather than courts — i.e., the same channel that already rejected this app.
- UFC: name "UFC", event names and fighter names/records in text are facts/nominative; the UFC logo and "Octagon" are red. Documented enforcement is piracy and licensing disputes; I found no action against a stats/scores app, so residual risk is low-moderate and logo-driven.
- Olympics: the app does not list Olympic sports, but if it ever adds them, the Ted Stevens Act regime (no confusion showing required) makes even plain-text "Olympic" in branding a red item; reporting results under the words "Olympic Games" is still protected news use. Keep this out of scope unless the roadmap changes.
- UEFA (Champions League): not specifically researched; treat like Premier League for crests/data, with the added point that the UEFA "starball" and anthem are heavily protected.

### Gaps
- No documented F1 action against a third-party scores/timing *app* was found; the closest are domain disputes and creator C&Ds. One forum post about a third-party live-timing app disappearing after a rights change is unverified — [Windows Central forum](https://forums.windowscentral.com/threads/formula-1-app.211865/page-2).
- No UEFA- or Olympic-specific 2024–2026 enforcement against apps was researched beyond the SFAA v. USOC baseline.
- No Serie A or La Liga trademark (as opposed to piracy) enforcement against a data app was found.

## Element-by-element classification (green / gray / red) with authorities and quantified risk

### Takeaway
Strip logos, crests and ESPN-hosted photos; keep names, abbreviations, colours (as a UI convention, not a badge), scores, schedules, stats and licensed odds; wall the BetUS unit off from league/team marks and geo-fence or remove its links. That configuration is defensible under NBA v. Motorola, New Kids/Tabari/Lendingtree and CBC/Daniels, and is what Apple's 5.2.1/5.2.2/5.3 review is actually asking for.

### Inferences
- The council's practical sequence: (1) remove every logo/crest/shield and every ESPN-hosted image; (2) ship names + abbreviations + colour chips with a generic shape; (3) add a global non-affiliation disclaimer and keep league/team names out of the app name, icon and sponsor unit; (4) obtain and file the Odds API terms and a licensed scores/schedule feed agreement so there is a document to hand Apple under 5.2.2; (5) either drop the BetUS link/placement or geo-fence it out of regulated states and remove any league/team marks from the sponsored unit; (6) resubmit with a reviewer note explaining the data sources and that marks are used nominatively.
- The step that most changes the legal posture is (5), not (1). Logos are an Apple problem with a modest league tail; the offshore sponsorship is the item with regulators actively sending letters in 2025 and with leagues' strongest motive to litigate.

### Gaps
- Risk percentages are professional judgment from the enforcement record found; there is no published base rate for C&Ds against scores apps.
- Several primary documents could not be fetched through the research proxy (Disney Terms of Use English text; Fifth Circuit Smack Apparel PDF; OpenJurist mirror); their holdings are cited from the court URL plus multiple secondary summaries and should be pulled directly before any external reliance.
