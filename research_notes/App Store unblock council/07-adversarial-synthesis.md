# 07 — Adversarial round and reconciled synthesis

App Store unblock council, Nosebleed Sports. State as of 2026-10-19.

Method note. This document works only from memos 01–05 in this folder; no new research was run. Where two memos disagree on a fact, the one whose memo cites a primary page it actually fetched outranks one working from a search summary, which outranks an unflagged inference; each memo's own "via search summary" / "estimate" / "inference" flags were used to make that call. Quotations are from the memos as written. Hard constraint applied throughout: every path must be legally defensible — no evasion, no ToS-violating scraping, no "until we get caught."

## 1. Adversarial round

Twenty attacks, grouped by attacker. Each names the claim, says why it is weak, and states what the attacked persona should concede.

### Attacker 01 — App Store Review Specialist

**01 → 02 (IP attorney): the "GRAY for web / RED for iOS" split on the ESPN proxy.** Memo 02 classifies server-side proxying of ESPN's undocumented JSON as "GRAY for the web app; RED for the iOS submission because 5.2.2 requires written permission 'upon request' that does not exist." From App Review's side the split is illusory: the iOS build is the same `server.js` and the same feed, so a reviewer who asks for authorization is asking about the company's data practice, not a platform. More important, the council's brief rules out ToS-violating scraping, so "gray" (a litigation probability) is not a usable category, and memo 02 concedes it never read the English Disney Terms of Use (its clause comes from a 2016 Spanish PDF and a third-party tracker). Concede: ESPN JSON is not permitted on either surface pending the actual ToS text; the only honest classification under this brief is "off until a licensed feed replaces it."

**01 → 03 (data exec): "the same company that denied-by-silence or declined Nosebleed."** Memo 03 names Sportradar as the denying party and builds on it ("A single 'no' from Sportradar now closes official doors for 4+ of Nosebleed's sports at once"). The brief says the denying party is unknown, and memo 04 states "No source confirms the specific reason Nosebleed was denied." This is an inference presented as fact, and it skews the re-approach strategy toward one vendor's terms. Concede: the identity and stated reason of the denial are unknown until the founder produces the correspondence; the exec's consolidation point (Sportradar now controls PGA/WTA/MLS/EuroLeague official data) stands on its own.

**01 → 04 (compliance): "the Whop link rejection deserves a direct appeal" and "file Apple appeal citing 3.1.1(a)."** Apple's own staff guidance, quoted in memo 01 from thread 786489, is to respond to information requests before appealing, one appeal per rejection, for cases where the reviewer misapplied the rule. The logo ground is valid, so the binary must change regardless; filing a Board appeal on the original build spends the single appeal on a half-fixed app and delays the clock. Concede: the correct vehicle is a Resolution Center reply attached to a new build that cites 3.1.1/3.1.1(a) verbatim; reserve the Board appeal for a second 3.1.1 rejection of a US-only build.

**01 → 05 (engineer): ranking Option 3 (hide the CTA) above Option 2 (US link-out) because "3.1.3(b) requires the item to also be available as IAP" and the card implies "features unlocked by an external mechanism."** 3.1.3(b) governs access *inside the app* to content bought elsewhere; memo 01's codebase reading found "no sign-in/account surface in `index.html`, so a web purchaser cannot unlock anything *inside* the app." Nothing is unlocked in-app, so 3.1.3(b) is not engaged, and 3.1.1(a) says verbatim that entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps." The engineer's Option 2 estimate also double-counts: US-only availability is an App Store Connect setting, so the native storefront gate is unnecessary. Concede: Option 2 is lawful today and costs about one incremental day; the legitimate residue is the card copy ("Unlock the Warroom →"), which reads like an in-app unlock and should be rewritten as a plain informational link.

### Attacker 02 — Sports IP Attorney

**02 → 01 (App Review): the "rights file" that lists "ESPN as scores source."** Memo 01 advises: "Prepare, but do not submit, a rights file: data-source list (odds API license, ESPN as scores source), and a short counsel letter on nominative use." A document whose purpose is to prove "authorization upon request" under 5.2.2 cannot name a source for which no authorization exists; handing it over converts a question into an admission. Memo 01's own gap says "a licensed scores feed is the only fully defensible answer." Concede: the resubmitted build must not depend on ESPN at all, and the rights file lists only sources whose terms the team has read and that permit the use.

**02 → 03 (data exec): "The market will sell Nosebleed scores and odds today, lawfully" from API-Sports and balldontlie.** The same memo could not read either vendor's terms ("Treat their betting posture as 'unknown, not prohibited in anything surfaced'") and says balldontlie's provenance is "undisclosed." "Lawfully" requires a contract you have read; and a vendor that will not say how it collects may be re-serving the same ESPN or league endpoints, which launders the problem rather than solving it (Morris: the league controls the pipe, not the facts). Concede: "lawful" is conditional on (a) reading the ToS for commercial display in a sponsored, betting-adjacent app and (b) a provenance statement; a vendor that refuses both is not a fix.

**02 → 04 (compliance): "3–6 months of demonstrably clean operation before reapplying."** Memo 04 labels this ESTIMATE and admits "No source documents how long a denied media applicant waited." Applied generally it conflicts with memo 04's own advice that SportsDataIO is "the realistic first stop," because SportsDataIO sells use-case-scoped commercial licenses and openly courts affiliates — no probation is needed there. The waiting period, if real, applies only to the official tier (Sportradar media addendum, Genius), and delaying the licensed-feed contract delays the one document Apple asks for under 5.2.2. Concede: contract a secondary licensed scores feed now; the "clean since" clock matters for Sportradar/Genius only.

**02 → 05 (engineer): the LEAN scenario keeps ESPN as a "'tolerated-risk' tier, not fully ToS-clean ... a dated bridge with an exit date."** That is "until we get caught" in engineering vocabulary, and the brief forbids it. Memo 05's own volume model makes it worse: 16 ESPN paths at 45 s is "≈921,600 calls/month regardless of season," which is the automated-copying fact pattern Disney's terms describe, and if ESPN blocks and the proxy adapts, Ryanair v. Booking.com shows a jury will find CFAA intent. Concede: LEAN is not a council option; Scenario A ships on licensed feeds for the sports launched, and sports without an affordable licensed feed launch as odds/schedule-only or not at all.

### Attacker 03 — Sports Data Industry Exec

**03 → 01 (App Review): "the only defensible 'permission' path is to license logos (e.g., from a sports-data vendor that sublicenses official marks) — slow and expensive — or to remove them."** No such vendor exists for this app. Memo 03's matrix found exactly two image sublicensors: Sportradar Images (team/league logos, "for media use only," "prohibited for betting clients," licensed per sport) and SportsDataIO (headshots only, via USA Today/Imagn, quote-only; its team-logo field is a Wikipedia URL). API-Sports and Sportmonks disclaim logo rights outright. Presenting "license logos" as a path, even a slow one, overstates the market. Concede: for Nosebleed as configured there is no logo license at any price; the only path is removal, and that should be stated to the founder without a hedge.

**03 → 02 (IP attorney): "ESPN/Yahoo/theScore are licensed (ESPN is a league broadcaster; theScore holds an NFL partner licence in Canada)."** The cited theScore item is theScore *Bet*'s 2026 Ontario/Alberta marketing designation, not a logo license for the theScore media app; "ESPN is a league broadcaster" is not a finding about logo clearance; and memo 02's own gap concedes it could not verify Sofascore/Flashscore/365Scores/Action Network. This is inference presented as fact. Concede: the right statement is "we do not know how competitors clear logos, and it does not matter, because Apple is the gate and asks for your paper, not theirs."

**03 → 04 (compliance): SportsDataIO as "the realistic first stop," placed at "Month 4–9."** Memo 03 found SportsDataIO's real-time production tier is quote-based with a third-party reported median of $16,496/yr — about five times the sub-$250/month envelope — and its $99 self-serve tier lacks live play-by-play. The memo is right on policy (use-case-scoped, affiliate-tolerant) and silent on price, which is the binding constraint. Concede: SportsDataIO is a quote to request in week 1, not a plan to rely on; the near-term scores feed is a cheaper secondary vendor whose ToS passes legal review.

**03 → 05 (engineer): "PGA/WTA/F1/UFC → Sportradar single-sport trials to validate the adapters, then a quote."** Sportradar trial keys are "specific to a single sport," 1,000 calls per 30 days, and the US developer terms require "express written approval" for any use "in connection with a gambling or betting business" — approval Sportradar "can grant or withhold at its discretion" and has every post-Muddy-Waters incentive to withhold from a picks app. Using a trial to validate an adapter you cannot license is wasted days. Concede: PGA leaderboards, F1, WTA live and UFC live are out of v1 scope; The Odds API outrights cover golf/tennis odds, and memo 05 already notes there is "no F1 key in `LEAGUE_SPORTS`."

### Attacker 04 — Gaming Compliance Officer

**04 → 01 (App Review): "show odds only from US-licensed operators ... or to strip the outbound `book-link` hrefs entirely," presented as interchangeable fixes.** Linking to licensed books is not cost-free: revenue-share affiliate links trigger major-vendor licensing (Colorado $11,200 upfront plus hourly investigation; NJ CSIE-level; PA registration with officer fingerprints), FTC material-connection disclosures, and — for the Android build memo 04 researched — Google Play lists "a dedicated sports odds tracker app containing integrated gambling ads linking to a sports betting site" as a violation. Concede: the resubmission carries *no* outbound sportsbook links; licensed-book links come later, after registrations, with disclosures, as a separate release.

**04 → 02 (IP attorney): "show BetUS odds as one row of data only if the row is not paid and not linked" (GRAY).** This ignores two documents in the record: NJ DGE's media guidance (memo 03) that an unlicensed site, if mentioned at all, must be labeled "not licensed to provide sports wagering bets in New Jersey," and FanDuel's affiliate rule that promotion of offshore sites "alongside ads for FanDuel" is grounds for termination. An odds board that lists BetUS, Bovada, MyBookie, BetOnline and LowVig prices next to DraftKings is the surface memo 04 called "an offshore referral surface," paid or not. Concede: filter every offshore book out of the board via a bookmaker allowlist; the "unpaid unlinked row" is a judgment call the council does not need to make.

**04 → 03 (data exec): The Odds API is "Low" risk because "the vendor explicitly carries offshore books and disclaims operator status."** A vendor's disclaimer shifts the burden, it does not remove it — memo 03's own reading of the terms says compliance with responsible-gambling and advertising rules is the user's "sole responsibility." Carrying a `betus` key is not a license to show BetUS prices to a user in Michigan, where the MGCB ordered BetUS out in May 2025. And that reading of the terms is a search summary; memo 02 could not retrieve the clause text. Concede: The Odds API is low-risk *after* allowlisting to US-licensed books and *after* the terms are read; its offshore coverage is a liability feature, not a safety signal.

**04 → 05 (engineer): the 13 `BOOK_LINKS` deep-links as a "5.3.4 watch-out" that "the Apple-policy persona should pre-empt ... in the review notes."** Five of those thirteen domains (betus.com.pa, bovada.lv, mybookie.ag, betonline.ag, lowvig.ag) are operators named in 2024–2026 state cease-and-desists that characterize their US business as felonies; no Review Notes paragraph pre-empts that. Scenario A has no effort line for bookmaker filtering or unlinking at all. Concede: add a line (about 0.5 day) for a server-side bookmaker allowlist and client-side unlinking, and treat it as a resubmission blocker, not a note.

### Attacker 05 — Indie Sports App Engineer

**05 → 01 (App Review): "the change is a config flip plus removing `LEAGUE_LOGOS` and the headshot provider."** Memo 01 read `app.js` but not `providers/logos.js` or `providers/history.js`. There are six image code paths (three server providers, three client helpers); `history.js` emits an ESPN `logo` href per competitor; `brandChipHtml()` pulls Google favicons; a stale cached `/api/logos` payload can leak images after the flag flips; and the team abbreviations themselves come from ESPN's teams directory, so "no ESPN call" requires a static `data/teams.json`. Concede: 2.5–3 person-days plus a curated palette, and abbreviations need a non-ESPN source before the build is clean.

**05 → 02 (IP attorney): "replace ESPN JSON with a licensed data API (the Odds API already covers odds; a licensed scores/schedule feed such as Sportradar, Genius, SportsDataIO, API-Sports, etc.) whose terms expressly grant display rights."** Of that list, Sportradar is trial-only until a sales contract and needs written approval for gambling-connected use; Genius has no developer portal; SportsDataIO is quote-based; no self-serve vendor with published pricing covers PGA, WTA, F1 or UFC live; The Odds API has no F1 key and golf is outrights-only. "A licensed feed" at indie prices is four or five sports, not fourteen. Concede: the legal classification must be mapped onto the sport list that can actually be licensed, and the launch scope narrows accordingly.

**05 → 03 (data exec): "API-Sports and balldontlie cover the whole list for ~$150–300/mo."** Memo 03's own matrix says API-Sports has "No tennis/golf"; balldontlie's All-Access price conflicts across three sources ($159.99/$299.99/$499.99) and its GOAT tier is per sport ($39.99 each, so five sports is $200 before odds); API-Sports Pro tiers (5,000–7,500 req/day) do not cover naive 60 s polling across sports with odds; and neither vendor's ToS was read. Concede: the realistic run-rate is $200–450/month for the sports actually launched, with The Odds API's paid tier on top, and the first production week must replace estimates with the `meta.usage` headers the server already captures.

**05 → 04 (compliance): "if the rejection cited the Whop link it is most likely either (a) the reviewer treated the 'Betting Pass' as real-money gaming (triggering 5.3.3/5.3.4 ...)."** Memo 04 admits "Apple's exact rejection text for Nosebleed Live was not provided to me; my mapping ... is inference." The brief describes the ground as selling the pass "through an external Whop checkout link," which is 3.1.1 vocabulary, not 5.3; the cheaper explanation (memos 01 and 05) is that the build was available outside the US storefront, which is a settings check. "Most likely" is unsupported and would send the team renaming a product instead of flipping availability. Concede: do nothing to the product's name or description until the verbatim rejection text is read; check App Store Connect availability first.

## 3. Contradictions resolved

**Is the Whop link-out lawful on the US storefront, and why does the engineer rank hiding it higher?** Lawful. Memo 01 quotes the October 16, 2026 guideline text directly: entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps," and 3.1.3 carves the US storefront out of the no-steering rule. Memo 05's ranking rests on 3.1.3(b), which governs in-app access to content bought elsewhere; since the app has no sign-in and unlocks nothing in-app, it is not engaged. The engineer's ranking is a review-friction preference, not a legal one, and its cost estimate for Option 2 includes a native storefront gate that is unnecessary once availability is set to the US in App Store Connect. Resolution: resubmit US-only with the link kept, behind a feature flag, with the card copy changed from "Members Only / Unlock the Warroom →" to a plain informational link that opens Safari. If Apple rejects a US-only build on 3.1.1 again, flip the flag (Option 3) and resubmit the same day rather than argue. Build IAP plus sign-in (Option 1) behind it, because the US link-out regime depends on litigation (below).

**Is the 15% a ruling or a proposal?** A proposal. Memo 01, with the most sources and dates, is explicit: Apple filed a remand "proffer" in August 2026 proposing up to 15%; Epic rejects any rate above 0%; the Ninth Circuit (Dec 2025) held only that a total bar on commission was overbroad and sent the rate back to the district court; the Supreme Court granted cert (June 2026) on the contempt standard only, with argument not before 2027. Memo 05 cites Apple's earlier 27% demand, which is historical. All of this is via search summaries of court filings. Resolution: model 0% today and 0–15% in 2027; nothing in the record says a court-set rate would be retroactive, but no source addressed it.

**Is BetUS on social-only acceptable?** No, for two different reasons, and one persona took no position. Memo 01's scope was the binary ("drop the BetUS sponsor placement in iOS"), which is all Apple needs. Memo 04 shows that data providers review the brand ("Nosebleed Sports" applies, not the app), that Massachusetts's 2025 letters attacked marketing "using U.S. teams, players, and content creators," and that NJ's 2015 template attached the taint to "principals." Memo 02 classifies a paid, geo-unrestricted BetUS placement as RED and says "'Show it everywhere and see' is not an option I can endorse." Memo 03 says reapplication is realistic only with BetUS off "the surface." Resolution: for App Review, removal from the app suffices; for legal defensibility and for the data re-approach, paid promotion of a state-C&D'd operator to a US audience through 44 accounts without geo-fencing is the "promoting gambling" fact pattern, and no persona found a way to make it defensible. Wind BetUS down everywhere, on the contract's own terms (see Section 4c).

**Is ESPN proxying "gray" for web but "red" for iOS?** The split falls. Memo 02's "gray" measures the chance of a letter; memo 03 flags the same feed as ToS-violating; memo 05 calls it "not fully ToS-clean." Under the brief's constraint, permission is the test, and no memo found permission (Disney's terms, as summarized, bar automated access and copying; ESPN closed its API program in 2014). The iOS and web apps share `server.js`. Resolution: ESPN JSON and ESPN-hosted images come out of the server for both surfaces, on the same date. The one caveat: the English Disney Terms of Use were never fetched; counsel pulls them on day 1 so the file can be closed on the actual text.

**Does any provider lawfully sublicense logos?** Not to Nosebleed. Memo 03's matrix (the most complete on this point) found Sportradar Images (team/league logos, "media use only," "prohibited for betting clients") and SportsDataIO (headshots via USA Today/Imagn, quote-only; team logos as Wikipedia URLs). API-Sports and Sportmonks disclaim rights. Memo 01's "license logos from a vendor" and memo 02's "theScore is licensed" both fall. Resolution: ship with no marks; headshots are the one image class that could be bought later, at a price that will likely exceed the data budget on its own.

**Is reapplication to the data providers realistic?** Partly, and it depends on which provider. Memo 03: "plausible, not proven," with no documented precedent of a media company re-approved after dropping offshore promotion. Memo 04: SportsDataIO first (commercial, use-case-scoped, affiliate-tolerant), then Sportradar's media tier (whose addendum reads "media and editorial purposes and non-commercial use only" — the sticking point for a paid-picks app), then Genius only through a licensed-operator partnership. Resolution: SportsDataIO-type commercial vendors — realistic in weeks once BetUS is gone and the evidence package exists, subject to price. Sportradar media tier — plausible, expect a "non-commercial" objection and a "come back with a clean record" answer. Genius — not realistic for an independent picks app. Since the denying party is unknown, the email in Section 6 is written to all three.

**Appeal or resubmit?** Resubmit (memo 01 over memo 04). See attack 01 → 04.

**Why was the Whop link rejected?** Unknown. Three hypotheses are in the record: availability outside the US storefront (01, 05), reviewer misreading (01), the pass read as real-money-gaming credit (04). The verbatim rejection text and the App Store Connect availability settings decide it; nothing else should be changed until they are read.

**What do The Odds API's terms say?** Memo 03 (search extract of the terms page) is better sourced than memo 02 ("not retrievable"), so the council provisionally accepts: not a wagering operator; compliance is the user's responsibility; attribution not required; no resale as a competing feed. Memo 03 also reports BetUS, Bovada, BetOnline and MyBookie as bookmaker keys under the `us` region, which is why allowlisting is required. The document must be pulled and the account's plan and quota confirmed.

**Can one affordable provider cover all fourteen sports?** No (memo 05 over memo 03's summary line). The Odds API already supplies scores and odds for the big four, college football and basketball, and soccer, at about a 30 s refresh; what ESPN removal costs is clocks/periods, box scores, history and images. Golf is outrights-only, F1 has no key, and no self-serve vendor with published pricing covers PGA/WTA/F1/UFC live.

**Should v1 link to US-licensed books?** No (memo 04 over memo 01). No outbound sportsbook links in the resubmission; add them in a later release after affiliate registrations and FTC disclosures.

## 4. Reconciled synthesis

**(a) The fastest compliant resubmission.** One build, US storefront only, submitted when the following are all true. Every league shield, team logo, club crest, player headshot and country-flag image is gone from the binary, the server, the metadata and the screenshots; teams are name, abbreviation and an app-authored color chip in a generic shape (not a badge, not the team's wordmark font), with abbreviations from a static `data/teams.json` rather than ESPN. League names stay as plain sport labels inside the UI and out of the app name, subtitle, keywords and icon. A visible non-affiliation line ("Not affiliated with or endorsed by any league, team or player; names are used for identification only") is added. The odds board is filtered server-side to an allowlist of US-licensed books; every `book-link` href is removed (unlinked labels only); the Polymarket and Kalshi links are off in the iOS build; the BetUS sponsor unit is gone from the app. The ESPN proxy is switched off for line scores, box scores, history, logos, headshots and news, and the launch sport list is whatever The Odds API (on a paid tier whose terms counsel has read) and one licensed secondary vendor (whose terms counsel has read) actually cover; sports without a licensed live source ship as odds/schedule-only or are hidden. The locked-picks card stays, behind a flag, reworded as an informational link that opens Safari; Review Notes quote 3.1.1(a). The wrapper is Capacitor with bundled assets and at least push notifications for followed games, a local pre-game reminder and native share, described specifically in Review Notes. Age rating answered honestly (expect 18+); privacy-policy and support URLs set; "verified picks" becomes "expert picks" unless a tracked record exists. Review Notes follow memo 01's draft, minus any mention of ESPN. If Apple returns a 3.1.1 rejection on this US-only build, hide the card and resubmit the same day; do not appeal. If Apple asks for data authorization under 5.2.2, hand over The Odds API terms and the scores vendor's terms — which is why those contracts precede submission.

**(b) The data stack with zero league licenses.** Odds and scores: The Odds API, paid tier sized from the live-gated model (scores only while a key has a game in progress, odds every five minutes, h2h in list view), with `meta.usage` headers replacing estimates after a week. Clocks, box scores and standings: one licensed secondary vendor for the big four plus soccer, chosen by counsel's reading of the ToS (candidates in order of published price: Sportmonks Starter for soccer; balldontlie GOAT per sport or API-Sports per sport for NBA/NFL/MLB/NHL), with a provenance statement requested in writing. SportsDataIO: request a quote in week 1 for real-time big-four plus NCAA and the Imagn headshot add-on; treat as a later upgrade.
